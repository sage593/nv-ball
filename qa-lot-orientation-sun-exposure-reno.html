For buyers · The compass check

Lot orientation: the house-hunting tool nobody packs

At this latitude and altitude, which way a house faces quietly writes its daily life, and locals read it like a disclosure: south-facing driveways melt their own snow and ice while north-facing ones hold both for weeks in the shade (the single most practical orientation fact in the region — walk an older neighborhood in January and the pattern is painted in ice), west-facing windows and bedrooms collect ferocious late-afternoon summer heat (the high-desert sun at altitude is stronger than the thermometer admits), south glass is free winter heating done right (the efficiency file counts it), and backyard orientation interacts with the afternoon wind to set whether evenings outside actually happen. Ten seconds with a phone compass at every showing; here's what to read.

Add the compass check

The winter read

South-facing driveways and entries self-clear after storms; north-facing ones bank ice in the shade line for weeks — real daily-life difference for steep driveways especially (the foothill addresses compound it). Roof exposure follows the same sun: south planes shed, north planes hold. January neighborhoods display all of it honestly; summer buyers borrow the knowledge with a compass.

The summer read

West-facing glass takes the full late-afternoon blast — bedrooms that won't cool until midnight, patios unusable at dinner — while east-facing versions get gentle morning light and shaded evenings. Mitigations exist (trees, awnings, films) and cost money the orientation got free. The checklist question at every showing: where is the sun at 5 PM in July, and which rooms are in its way?

The composite read

The locally prized combination: south-facing front (winter driveway) with east-or-south backyard (evening shade, morning patio light, wind shelter where planting helps) and main living spaces lit south — and solar-curious buyers add roof planes to the read (the solar file). None of it vetoes a great house; all of it prices mitigation honestly and explains why identical floor plans live differently across a street.

Straight answers

Does the direction a house faces matter in Reno?

Yes — south driveways self-clear winter ice (north ones bank it for weeks), west glass bakes on summer afternoons, south glass heats winters free, and backyard orientation + the afternoon wind writes the patio schedule. Bring a compass.

Is a north-facing house bad to buy?

Not bad — priced: icier driveway (steep ones especially), but a south-lit backyard and cooler summers. Every orientation trades; know yours.

What's the best orientation for a backyard here?

East or south: evening shade, morning light, friendlier wind. West takes the late blast; mitigation costs what orientation gives others free. Tour at your real patio hour.
