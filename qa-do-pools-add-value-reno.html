For buyers & sellers · The backyard question

Do pools add value to a home in Reno?

The honest high-desert answer: sometimes, to some buyers, and rarely what they cost to build. Reno's short-but-hot swim season splits the market — some buyers pay a genuine premium for summer-ready backyards, while others subtract maintenance, winterization, and safety concerns from their offers. Pool homes here sell well when marketed to the first group and priced without fantasy; they linger when sellers expect construction-cost recovery. NV Ball Real Estate Group sells both pool and no-pool versions of the same neighborhoods — here's what the market actually says.

Price my backyard honestly

The two buyer camps

Premium-payers: entertainers, hot-summer families, buyers from pool cultures (Vegas, Phoenix, SoCal arrivals know pool life). Discounters: maintenance-wary, small-children-cautious, and anyone doing winterization math. Marketing a pool home means finding camp one; pricing means not pretending camp two doesn't exist.

The seller reality

Expect a contribution to value, not cost recovery — pools are lifestyle purchases, not investments, and appraisers treat them modestly. What helps: equipment records, recent service, safety compliance (fencing, covers), and summer-season listing timing when the backyard sells itself.

The buyer diligence

Pool-specific inspection (shell, equipment, leaks — a specialist's job, not the general inspector's), operating-cost honesty (power, chemicals, service, winterizing in our climate), and safety-code review. We order the specialist look on every pool purchase — surprises here run expensive.

Straight answers

Do pools add value to homes in Reno?

Modestly, conditionally — some buyers pay up, others discount, appraisers credit well below build cost. Marketed to the right camp and priced for contribution (not cost recovery), pool homes sell fine here.

Is a pool a problem in Reno's climate?

A commitment, not a problem: real June–September seasons, annual winterization, freeze-aware equipment. Happy pool owners did the operating math first; regretful ones skipped it.

Should I buy a house with a pool here?

If your Saturdays want one — with a specialist inspection, honest operating math, and safety review. Lukewarm? Buy the no-pool house; lukewarm owners fund the resurfacing industry.
