Relocation · The body's move-in

Adjusting to Reno's altitude and dry climate: what to expect

Reno sits near 4,500 feet in genuine high desert — mild numbers by mountain standards, real enough that coastal arrivals feel the difference: drier skin and sinuses within days, harder-hitting sun and alcohol, occasionally lighter sleep and breath in the first weeks, and a dehydration baseline that catches people who drink water like they still live at sea level. The playbook is boring and effective — water, humidifier, lotion, sunscreen, patience — and most bodies recalibrate within weeks. The planning-ahead cases are real too: certain respiratory and cardiac situations deserve a physician conversation before, not after, the move. Here's the honest field guide.

Settle in comfortably

What the first month feels like

Thirstier than makes sense, skin and sinuses announcing the humidity drop, sun with surprising teeth (elevation thins the filter), drinks landing harder, and sometimes lighter early sleep. All standard, all temporary for most — the recalibration typically runs days-to-weeks, not months.

The boring playbook

Water beyond your old baseline, a bedroom humidifier (the single highest-leverage purchase), lotion and lip balm in every bag, year-round sunscreen, moderated alcohol while adjusting, and patience with workouts — performance returns as the body adds its adaptations. Tahoe trips (another 1,700+ feet) deserve the same respect squared.

Who should plan ahead

Most people need no planning — but certain respiratory and cardiac conditions interact with altitude in ways worth a physician's input before relocating, and continuity of care deserves arranging regardless. The honest line: this page is lived experience, not medicine; your doctor owns the medical questions.

Straight answers

Do people have trouble adjusting to Reno's altitude?

Mildly, briefly, mostly: thirst, dry skin/sinuses, toothier sun, harder-landing drinks, sometimes lighter sleep — weeks, not months. Playbook: water, humidifier, sunscreen, patience. Medical conditions: physician first.

Is the dry climate good or bad for you?

A trade: humidity-aggravated conditions often improve; skin and sinuses pay the tax until routines adjust. The humidifier is the peace treaty.

Does the altitude affect visiting family and guests?

Same effects, visit-sized: sun and drinks hit guests harder, and Tahoe day trips deserve water-first planning. Stock the guest room accordingly.
