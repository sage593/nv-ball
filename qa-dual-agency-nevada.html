Can one agent represent both sides in Nevada? Dual agency, explained

Nevada allows it — with informed written consent: when one licensee would act for both buyer and seller, state law requires disclosure through the Duties Owed form and your signed Consent to Act before proceeding. What the form can't change is the arithmetic: an agent serving both sides can't fully advocate for either — no advice on what to offer, no coaching on counters. Sometimes consenting is reasonable (simple deals, informed parties); often, separate representation serves you better. NV Ball Real Estate Group explains the trade before asking for any signature — here's the whole picture.

Know who works for you

Nevada's framework

Every client gets the Duties Owed by a Nevada Real Estate Licensee form naming who represents whom; a licensee acting for both sides needs the Consent to Act signed by both. The paperwork is real consumer protection — but only if you understand what you're consenting to, which is the part we slow down for.

What dual agency costs you

Advocacy: a dual agent can't advise either side on offer price, negotiation strategy, or repair demands without betraying the other. The transaction gets a referee instead of two coaches — fine for some deals, expensive for contested ones.

When to consent, when to decline

Reasonable: clean transactions, experienced parties, or in-brokerage situations with separate agents (a different arrangement than true dual agency — ask which yours is). Decline when you need a negotiator: contested pricing, repair fights, or any deal where advice is the point of having an agent.

Straight answers

Is dual agency legal in Nevada?

Legal with signed consent — the Duties Owed and Consent to Act forms make it official. The real question is the trade: a dual agent can't advise either side on price or strategy. Convenience in; advocacy out.

The listing agent says they can write my offer — should I let them?

Only after understanding the trade: fairness, yes; advice on price or strategy, no. On anything contested or substantial, your own agent is the coach the deal deserves — usually at no extra cost to you.

What's the difference between dual agency and two agents at one brokerage?

Different animals: two agents under one roof can each advocate; one agent serving both can't. Nevada's forms cover both — ask which you're signing. We show clients on the form itself.
