Buying · The fundamental fork, part two

New construction or resale in Reno?

The categories argue past each other; the specifics decide. New construction buys warranties, modern systems, efficiency, and design-center control — priced in lot premiums, build timelines, and HOA-governed newness. Resale buys established neighborhoods, mature trees, negotiating room, and known quantities — priced in older systems and finish compromises. We sell both happily, which is why we'll frame your actual alternatives instead of cheerleading a category.

Talk to an agent

Where new wins

Certainty of systems, warranty cover, your finishes from day one. The growth corridors deliver it: Spanish Springs, South Reno, Fernley.

Where resale wins

Trees, streets, and neighborhoods that took decades to settle — plus negotiating room new-build pricing rarely offers. Caughlin and Old Southwest make the case.

Either way

Representation and inspection, non-negotiable — at the sales office and at the century home alike. The homework differs; the rigor doesn't.

Straight answers

Should I buy new construction or a resale home in Reno?

New buys warranties, modern systems, and choice — at lot premiums, build timelines, and sameness. Resale buys established neighborhoods, mature trees, and negotiating room — at older systems and finish compromises. The specific alternatives, not the categories, decide.

What do new-build buyers underestimate?

The design-center budget, the need for their own agent at the sales office, and the independent inspection brand-new homes still deserve. Three classics, all avoidable.

What do resale buyers underestimate?

Upside: mature trees and settled streets cost decades to grow and nothing to buy. Caution: systems age — which is what our inspection rigor is for.
