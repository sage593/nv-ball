For sellers · Choosing a model

Flat-fee vs full-service realtor in Reno: an honest comparison

Flat-fee and limited-service listings can work — for experienced sellers with a simple, in-demand home who are ready to run showings, negotiation, and problem-solving themselves. Full service earns its fee when pricing judgment, preparation, marketing, and negotiation move the final price by more than the fee difference. NV Ball Real Estate Group is a full-service team, and we'll tell you plainly when your situation doesn't need everything we do.

Compare the models for my home

What flat-fee includes — and omits

Typically MLS entry and paperwork, with showings, pricing calls, negotiation, and crisis management staying yours. For some sellers that's fine; the model fails when a sale hits its first complication and nobody's on point.

Where full service pays

Pricing a computer can't see, prep that changes the photos, marketing that reaches past the MLS, and negotiation under pressure. The net sheet is where the comparison gets honest.

The test we invite

Ask us — and any flat-fee alternative — the same question: what will my net be, and what exactly do you do when something goes wrong in week three? Compare the answers, not the fees.

Straight answers

Should I use a flat-fee or full-service realtor in Reno?

Honest answer: it depends on the home and on how much of the job you want to own. Flat-fee can suit an experienced seller with a simple, in-demand home. Full service earns its keep when pricing judgment, prep, marketing, and negotiation move the price by more than the fee gap — on most homes, that's where the money actually is.

What do you actually get for a full-service commission?

Here: pricing judgment, prep and staging counsel, professional marketing, managed showings, negotiation on price and repairs, and a run-to-closing that doesn't drop balls — the discipline behind 93 homes sold in twelve months. Judge the fee by the net, not the sticker.

Can I negotiate commission in Nevada?

Yes — commissions are negotiable and always have been; recent industry changes just made it explicit. Any agent worth hiring explains cost and expected return in plain numbers. We put ours in writing before you sign.
