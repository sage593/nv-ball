For buyers & sellers · Second position

How do backup offers work in Nevada?

A backup offer is a signed contract in second position: if the first escrow fails, yours activates automatically — no re-negotiation, no race. For buyers who lost the house they wanted, it's a free option with real odds (escrows fail on financing, inspections, and cold feet more often than people think). For sellers, an accepted backup keeps the first buyer honest. NV Ball Real Estate Group writes and manages them on both sides.

Put me in position

For buyers: the free option

A backup costs nothing to hold — you can usually withdraw until it activates, and keep shopping meanwhile. When first escrows wobble, the seller's path of least resistance is the contract already signed: yours.

For sellers: the pressure tool

An accepted backup transforms every first-buyer renegotiation: 'we have a signed backup' is the strongest sentence in a repair-credit standoff. We solicit backups deliberately on hot listings.

The mechanics that matter

Position must be explicit, deposit handling clear, and your exit rights written — a sloppy backup can trap a buyer who found something better. Ours are written to protect the option, not just hold your place.

Straight answers

What is a backup offer and is it binding?

A signed contract in second position, auto-activating if escrow one dies. Binding once live — but written right, you can withdraw before activation. That's what makes it a free option worth holding.

What are the odds a backup actually gets the house?

Minority odds — but real. Escrows die of financing, inspections, and cold feet all the time, and when one does, there's no new bidding war: the house is simply yours.

Should I keep house-hunting while in backup position?

Yes. A properly drafted backup lets you exit for something better; we write them so the option runs in your favor, not just the seller's.
