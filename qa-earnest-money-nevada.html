Buying · Money

How much is earnest money in Nevada — and can I lose it?

Earnest money here commonly runs one to a few percent of the price — negotiable, not set by law — and it sits in escrow, never in the seller's pocket. The honest answer about losing it: you generally keep it when you cancel under a contingency your contract actually gives you (inspection, appraisal, financing), and you risk it when you walk away outside those protections. Which is why the deposit matters less than the contract wrapped around it.

Where it lives

In escrow, from day one — a neutral third party. The seller touches it only at closing (as part of your funds) or on a true buyer default.

The protection set

Inspection, appraisal, and financing contingencies with real dates. Cancel within them, deposit comes home. Our job is keeping those gates open as long as you need them.

The competitive lever

A larger deposit can strengthen an offer without raising real risk — if the contingencies stay intact. That balance is structure, not bravado.

Straight answers

How much is earnest money in Nevada and can I lose it?

Commonly one to a few percent — negotiable, held in escrow, not handed to the seller. You keep it when you cancel under a contingency your contract gives you; you risk it when you walk outside those protections. The dates and contingencies are the whole game, and we guard them.

When does earnest money actually get released to a seller?

Either at closing (it just counts toward your funds) or on a true default. Everything else is escrow's release process — not the seller deciding to keep it.

Can a bigger deposit win me the house?

Sometimes — it signals commitment. It only becomes dangerous when paired with stripped contingencies. We structure that balance deliberately, not emotionally.
