Buying · HOAs

What do HOA fees actually cover in Reno communities?

It depends on the community — and that dependence is the point. Dues might cover common-area landscaping, parks and trails, a pool or amenity center, private roads, even front yards or snow removal; in master-planned communities like Somersett or Damonte Ranch, a master association and a sub-association can each charge their own. Nevada law gives buyers a window to review the HOA's documents before being locked in. We make sure you actually use it.

The stack to check

Master HOA + sub-HOA is common in master-planned Reno. Two sets of dues, two rulebooks — we lay them side by side before you offer.

The documents that matter

CC&Rs (the rules), the budget, and the reserve study (the crystal ball for special assessments). They arrive in the resale package, and we read them with you.

Rules vs. lifestyle

Short-term rental limits, RV parking, paint palettes — deal-breakers for some buyers, irrelevant to others. Better to know which you are in escrow's first week, not its last.

Straight answers

What do HOA fees actually cover in Reno communities?

It genuinely varies — common areas, parks, amenity centers, sometimes private roads, front yards, or snow removal. Master-planned communities often stack a master HOA and a sub-HOA, each with dues. We read the documents with you before you commit, not after.

Are HOAs in Nevada strict?

Each one writes its own rulebook — some relaxed, some very particular about paint, RVs, and rentals. Nevada gives buyers a document-review window; our job is making sure you really use it.

Can HOA fees go up?

Yes — dues adjust, and big projects can bring special assessments. The budget and reserve study in the resale package are the tea leaves, and we read them with you.
