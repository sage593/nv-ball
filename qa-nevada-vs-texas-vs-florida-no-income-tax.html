Nevada vs Texas vs Florida: choosing among the no-income-tax states

The headline zero is identical; everything after diverges: Texas pairs its zero with some of the nation's heaviest property taxes (the quiet clawback on homeowners), Florida pairs its zero with a famously hardening property-insurance market (the number that reshapes budgets), and Nevada pairs its zero with moderate, increase-capped property taxes and an insurance picture that — wildfire-zone diligence aside — hasn't broken like coastal markets. Then the lifestyles diverge further: Gulf humidity versus Hill Country heat versus Sierra seasons. We're Nevada realtors — disclosure applies — so below we're specific about who should honestly pick the other two.

Sort my zero-tax fork

The after-the-zero taxes

Texas: property taxes among the nation's heaviest — the zero gets meaningfully clawed back through homeownership. Florida: moderate property taxes with homestead protections, but insurance premiums doing clawback work of their own. Nevada: moderate property taxes with annual-increase caps and an insurance market that still functions normally outside specific wildfire-interface homework. Your CPA models your version; the directions are durable.

The lifestyle fork

Florida: ocean, humidity, hurricane season, flat green abundance. Texas: scale, heat, big-metro depth across multiple cities. Nevada: four Sierra seasons, Tahoe, high-desert sun, and the twenty-minute metro. Climate preference sorts most households faster than spreadsheets do — and honestly should.

Who should pick the others

From Nevada realtors, plainly: ocean-organized lives belong in Florida; households needing multiple big-metro job markets or specific Texas industries belong there. Pick Nevada when mountains, seasons, moderate-tax stability, and California proximity organize your life — the honest assessment finishes the case.

Straight answers

Which no-income-tax state is best: Nevada, Texas, or Florida?

Same zero, different clawbacks: Texas via heavy property taxes, Florida via insurance, Nevada via neither (moderate, capped property taxes; functioning insurance). Then climate sorts: ocean-humid, big-hot, or four-season Sierra.

Is Nevada's total tax burden really lower than Texas's for homeowners?

Directionally yes — Texas's property-tax rates run among the nation's highest; Nevada's moderate and capped. On comparable values, Nevada's total usually lands lighter. CPA models your version; the direction holds.

What about Washington state — it has no income tax too?

True — paired with hub-metro housing costs and its own wrinkles (a capital-gains levy included). The NV-vs-WA fork usually resolves on housing math and sun; our Seattle guide runs it.
