Relocation · Taxes & work

Can I keep my California job and live in Nevada?

Usually, yes — it's one of the most common arrangements among our Bay Area relocation clients. The real question is how taxes follow the work: wages for days physically worked in California generally stay California-taxable, while work genuinely done from your Nevada home generally does not. The move has to be a true change of domicile, and the plan deserves a CPA's sign-off before the truck is loaded.

Talk to an agent

What changes

No Nevada state income tax on what you earn as a genuine Nevada resident working from Nevada. For many households this is the single biggest line item in the move.

What doesn't

Federal taxes, and California tax on California-source income — including days physically worked there and gains on California property. The border doesn't erase those.

What to document

When you moved, where you work each day, where your home, family, doctors, and registrations live. Clean records make residency questions boring — which is the goal.

Straight answers

Can I keep my California job and live in Nevada?

Often yes — a large share of our clients do exactly this. The key tax point: where you physically do the work matters. Days worked inside California are generally still California-taxable; work genuinely done from your Nevada home generally is not. Get a CPA to bless the plan before you move.

Does moving to Nevada automatically end my California taxes?

No — a Nevada address alone settles nothing. California weighs your whole picture, and its tax authority examines high earners who claim moves. Done properly and documented, the move holds; done casually, it invites questions.

How do people actually split the commute?

The common patterns: full-remote with periodic trips, or a few California days per month. RNO's Bay Area flights and the I-80 drive make both routine — ask us which neighborhoods make airport days easiest.
