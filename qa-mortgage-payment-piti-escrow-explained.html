For buyers & owners · The monthly anatomy

What's in a mortgage payment? PITI and escrow, explained

The payment has four parts — principal, interest, taxes, insurance (PITI) — and the last two explain nearly every post-closing surprise: your lender collects estimated taxes and insurance monthly into an escrow account, pays the bills when due, and reconciles annually. When taxes rise (new-owner adjustments are the Nevada classic) or insurance reprices, the annual escrow analysis adjusts your payment — sometimes with a shortage to repay on top. None of this means your rate changed; it means the estimate met reality. NV Ball Real Estate Group walks every buyer through this anatomy at closing so year-one letters surprise nobody.

Decode my payment

The four parts

Principal (the loan shrinking), interest (the loan's price), taxes (collected monthly, paid when due), insurance (same mechanics, plus any HOA paid separately). Fixed-rate loans fix the first two forever; the last two float with reality — which is why 'fixed payment' is only mostly true.

The escrow account's rhythm

Monthly collection, scheduled payouts, annual analysis: when the account's estimates miss (taxes adjusted, insurance repriced), the analysis trues it up — a new monthly amount, sometimes plus a shortage repayment. The letter reads alarming; the mechanics are boring. We translate on request, years after closing included.

The Nevada-specific surprise

New owners often see taxes adjust after purchase — the abatement-reset story — which flows straight into the escrow analysis as year-one's classic payment bump. Budgeting from the assessor's post-sale math at closing (we run it) prevents the whole surprise.

Straight answers

Why did my mortgage payment go up if I have a fixed rate?

Your rate fixes principal-and-interest only — taxes and insurance float through the escrow analysis. Nevada's classic: post-purchase tax adjustment hitting year one's analysis. Rate unchanged; estimates met reality.

What is an escrow account and do I need one?

The lender's collect-monthly, pay-when-due account for taxes and insurance — standard on most loans. Trade: forced budgeting vs. controlling your own cash. Most keep it.

What should I do with an escrow shortage letter?

Find the lever: taxes (check assessor + abatement status) or insurance (shop it). Then pick lump-sum vs. spread repayment. Arithmetic, not accusation — we translate on request.
