For sellers · The early exit

Selling a house after only 1–2 years: the honest math

Life happens — jobs move, families change, the house was wrong — and the early exit has two math problems worth facing squarely: transaction costs round-trip (buying costs plus selling costs rarely clear in a year or two unless the market moved for you), and the tax window (the federal primary-residence exclusion wants two years of ownership and residence; sell earlier and gains face capital-gains treatment, with partial exclusions available for qualifying reasons like job relocation). Sometimes the answer is still sell — a wrong house compounds daily. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs the real numbers before you decide; here's the framework.

Run my break-even

The break-even anatomy

What you paid to get in, what selling costs to get out, what the market did in between, and what you've paid down — four numbers that make the decision arithmetic instead of anguish. We build the net sheet first; feelings negotiate better with facts.

The tax window

Two years of owning AND living in the home unlocks the federal exclusion ($250k single / $500k married). Earlier sales face capital-gains treatment — with partial exclusions for qualifying moves (job relocation, health, certain unforeseen circumstances) your CPA can confirm. Sometimes waiting three months changes the whole picture; we'll flag it.

When selling early is still right

A wrong house costs daily — commute misery, space crises, payments that strangle. If the math says lose a little, the comparison is against the cost of staying wrong, not against a perfect world. And the alternatives get weighed honestly: renting it out sometimes bridges the gap to better timing.

Straight answers

Is there a penalty for selling a house before two years?

No fine — a tax window and a math problem. Under two years: the exclusion generally isn't yours (partial versions exist for qualifying moves), and round-trip costs often beat short appreciation. Run the real numbers; then decide.

How do I figure out if I'd lose money selling now?

The net sheet: realistic price − selling costs − payoff, versus what you put in. We build it free with live comps and call it plainly: gain, wash, or priced decision.

Should I rent it out instead of selling early?

Sometimes — a bridge to the window or better timing. Checks: TRUE rental math, landlord appetite, and the conversion's tax-clock effects. The rent-or-sell framework runs this fork.
