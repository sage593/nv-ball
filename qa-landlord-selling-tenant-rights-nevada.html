For renters · The other side of the sale

My landlord is selling the house I rent in Nevada — what are my rights?

The headline, honestly good news: in Nevada, a sale doesn't void your lease — a fixed-term lease generally survives and binds the new owner, your deposit transfers with its obligations, and showings require proper advance notice (Nevada law sets a minimum of 24 hours for entry). Month-to-month tenancies are the exposed case: they can be ended with the statutory notice by the old or new owner. We write this page as the team often standing on the other side — because sales go smoother when tenants know their actual rights, and because renters become buyers, often sooner than they planned.

Know where you stand

The lease survives

A fixed-term lease generally rides through the sale and binds the buyer — new owner, same terms, rent paid to a new address after proper notice. Check your lease for any early-termination-on-sale clause (uncommon, but they exist); absent one, your term is your term.

Showings and notice

Entry for showings requires advance notice — Nevada's statutory minimum is 24 hours, at reasonable times. Cooperative scheduling is negotiable, not mandatory servitude: many tenants trade flexible showing windows for consideration (gift cards, rent credit, moving help). That's a legitimate conversation; have it.

Month-to-month and the exit

Periodic tenancies can be ended with Nevada's statutory notice (30 days for most month-to-month situations) by either the seller or the buyer — which is the honest exposure. If you're facing it: get timelines in writing, ask about incentives (cash-for-keys arrangements are common and negotiable), and if buying has ever been on your mind, this moment is worth a conversation.

Straight answers

Can my landlord sell the house while I'm renting it in Nevada?

Yes — but your fixed-term lease survives and binds the buyer: same terms, deposit transferred, 24-hour minimum notice for showings. Month-to-month is the exposed case: endable with ~30 days' statutory notice.

Do I have to allow showings?

With proper 24-hour notice, yes — but cooperation beyond the minimum is worth something. Negotiate it: rent credit, gift cards, moving help. Smart sellers pay for cooperative showings.

The new owner wants to move in — what happens to me?

Lease: they wait out your term or pay for an early exit — negotiate it. Month-to-month: statutory notice applies; get timelines in writing and price your cooperation. Deposit obligations transferred; document on exit.
