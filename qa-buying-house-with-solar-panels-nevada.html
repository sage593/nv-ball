Buying a house with solar panels: the buyer's checklist

The seller's version of this page exists (we wrote it); here's yours: the ownership question still governs everything — owned systems convey as part of the house (verify: production history, warranties transferred, and title's check for solar-loan UCC filings that must clear at closing), while leased systems mean you're assuming someone's contract (qualification required, terms worth reading like the obligation they are, and walking away from the lease assumption is sometimes the right call that reshapes the deal). The Nevada layer: net-metering terms vary by when systems enrolled — the seller's utility bills tell the real production-and-credit story better than any brochure. Diligence, not romance; here's the list.

Check the roof's paperwork

Owned systems: verify the 'owned'

Solar loans ride on many 'owned' systems — title's UCC-filing check catches liens that must pay off or clear at closing (the week-one look that prevents week-four drama). Then the value file: production history (the utility bills), warranty transfers (panels, inverter, workmanship — transferable or not), and installer existence (warranties from the departed are stories).

Leased systems: read before assuming

You're inheriting a contract: assumption qualification (the solar company underwrites you), remaining term and escalators, buyout math, and what happens at the lease's end. Sometimes assuming is fine; sometimes negotiating seller buyout-at-closing reshapes the deal better. The lease document decides — read before loving.

The Nevada production story

Net-metering credit terms vary by enrollment vintage — the seller's actual utility bills (twelve months, requested in diligence) tell the production-and-savings truth no listing claim matches. And the honest baseline: Nevada sun makes real production likely; the paperwork decides whether it's your asset or someone's obligation.

Straight answers

What should I check when buying a house with solar panels?

Ownership first: 'owned' → UCC-filing check (loans clear at closing), real utility bills, warranty transfers; leased → read the contract (qualification, escalators, buyout) or negotiate seller buyout. Paperwork decides whose asset the roof is.

Do solar panels complicate the mortgage when buying?

Paid: no. Loaned: lien clears at closing (title finds it; negotiation prices it). Leased: a parallel assumption qualification. Early discovery keeps it boring.

How do I know if the solar actually saves money?

Twelve months of the seller's real bills vs. any riding costs. Bills beat brochures — we request them as standard.
