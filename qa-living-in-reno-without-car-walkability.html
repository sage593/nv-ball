Relocation · The car question

Can you live in Reno without a car? The honest answer

Mostly no — with two real exceptions and a popular middle: Reno is a car-shaped city where the RTC bus network provides coverage but not car-free convenience, and most neighborhoods assume driving the way coastal cities assume parking pain. The exceptions: Midtown and the downtown-university corridor support genuine walk-most-things living (groceries, restaurants, work for some), and they're exactly where car-free households concentrate. The popular middle: one-car households thrive broadly — one commuter car plus walkable-enough home base covers most two-adult lives here. NV Ball Real Estate Group answers the car question honestly before the neighborhood question — here's the map.

Map my car reality

Where car-free actually works

Midtown and the downtown-university corridor: walkable groceries, restaurant depth, campus and some employment in range, and the RTC's best-served routes. It's genuine — and it's two districts, not the metro.

The honest transit picture

RTC's buses cover the urban core creditably (commuter routes, the rapid Virginia Street line) and thin toward the suburbs and valleys; Tahoe and the outlying towns run on cars entirely. Transit here is a supplement and a backup — planning a car-free suburban life around it ends badly.

The one-car middle

The regional sweet spot: one commuter vehicle plus a walkable-enough base (Midtown's edges, the Wells corridor, downtown-adjacent Sparks) covers most two-adult households — halving the car budget without the car-free constraints. Remote workers make it work even wider; we design home searches around the actual driving math.

Straight answers

Can you live in Reno without a car?

Only in two districts, honestly: Midtown and the downtown-university corridor. Everywhere else is car-shaped; the valleys entirely. The popular middle: one-car households with a walkable base.

How good is public transit in Reno?

Core: creditable (Virginia Street spine, commuter routes). Edges: thin. A supplement that replaces cars only where walkability already lives.

Which neighborhoods are most walkable in Reno?

The short real list: Midtown (champion), University District, the Riverwalk core, old Sparks pockets. Everywhere else trades walkable for space — usually happily, one car kept.
