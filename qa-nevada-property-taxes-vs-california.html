Relocation · Taxes

How do Nevada property taxes compare to California's?

The structures differ, and for most people buying a home, Nevada's is the friendlier one: taxes are assessed locally and state law generally caps annual increases on an owner-occupied primary residence at 3%. California's Prop 13 shelters long-time owners — but the moment you buy a new California home, your basis resets to the new price. On comparable purchases, the Nevada bill usually tells the better story. For any specific home, the county assessor's figures are the real answer, and we pull them for every serious offer.

Straight answers

How do Nevada property taxes compare to California's?

Nevada assesses locally and generally caps annual increases on a primary residence at 3%. California's Prop 13 protects long-time owners, but buying new resets the basis — and on a comparable purchase, the Nevada bill is often the friendlier one. For a specific home, we pull the county assessor's actual figures.

Will my property tax jump after I buy in Nevada?

Increases on a primary residence are generally capped at 3% a year under Nevada's abatement law. We pull the assessor's current numbers for any home you're serious about.

Are there other Nevada taxes to know about?

Sales tax is real (varies by county) and transfers carry a tax — but no state income tax, no state estate tax, and retirement income untaxed. Most transplants come out well ahead; a CPA confirms your case.
