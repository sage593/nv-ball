Buying & selling · Money

Who pays closing costs in Nevada?

Both sides pay some — and more of it is negotiable custom than most people think. Buyers typically carry their loan-related costs; title and escrow fees are commonly split or negotiated; and Nevada's real property transfer tax customarily falls to the seller in many counties. Every one of those defaults can move in a negotiation, which is why the question isn't really 'who pays' but 'what did your agent negotiate.'

Straight answers

Who pays closing costs in Nevada?

Both sides pay some, and most of it is negotiable custom: buyers usually carry loan costs, title and escrow fees commonly split, and the transfer tax customarily lands on sellers in many counties — but all of it can move in negotiation. The escrow officer's estimate is the real number.

How much should a buyer budget for closing costs?

Plan roughly 2–4% of price as a financed buyer — then let your lender's official Loan Estimate replace the rough range within days of applying.

Can the seller pay my closing costs?

Often yes — seller credits are a normal lever here, within loan-type limits. Whether this month's market supports asking is exactly the kind of live read we give you.
