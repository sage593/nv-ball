For new owners · The post-closing discovery

I found a problem after closing — can I do anything about it?

The honest triage first: Nevada's disclosure framework makes sellers answerable for known defects they failed to disclose — not for problems they genuinely didn't know, not for items your inspection flagged and you accepted, and not for the ordinary aging of a used house. So the questions that matter: did the seller know (evidence: patches, paint-overs, neighbor accounts, repair records), was it disclosed or discoverable in your inspection file, and is the money at stake worth the path? Many discoveries resolve with a direct demand letter; some belong to attorneys; most, honestly, are new-house ownership arriving on schedule. NV Ball Real Estate Group walks past clients through this triage at no charge — here's the framework.

Triage it honestly

The liability line

Sellers answer for known-and-undisclosed — Nevada's disclosure form asks what they actually knew. The evidence that moves claims: concealment signs (fresh patches over problems, strategic paint), repair records contradicting 'no known issues,' neighbor and HOA accounts. Genuine ignorance and disclosed items don't create claims; concealment does.

The honest buckets

Bucket one: disclosed or inspection-flagged — you accepted it; it's yours. Bucket two: new failures of old components — aging, not deception. Bucket three: evidence of known-and-hidden — the real claim category. Most discoveries land in the first two; the triage's job is spotting the genuine thirds.

The escalation ladder

Documentation first (photos, bids, the disclosure and inspection files), the direct route second (a demand letter resolves more than people expect — sellers' memories improve when evidence arrives), attorneys third for real money and real concealment. We help past clients assemble the file and calibrate the rung — the disclosure guide explains what the form promised.

Straight answers

Can I sue the seller for problems found after closing in Nevada?

For the right category only: known-and-undisclosed. Not unknowns, not disclosed items, not ordinary aging. Triage: evidence of knowledge, your disclosure/inspection files, money-vs-path math. Demand letters outperform lawsuits.

What counts as evidence the seller knew?

Patches and strategic paint, contradicting repair records or permits, neighbor/HOA accounts, prior listings or claims. File first, claims second.

The problem is real but the seller probably didn't know — now what?

Then it's ownership — used houses surprise on schedule. Moves: good contractors, warranty claims where coverage exists, and the maintenance file. Not every discovery has a defendant.
