For buyers · The post-closing surprise

Why did my property taxes go up after buying a house in Nevada?

Because Nevada's tax-increase caps protect the bill, not the buyer: the state's partial abatement limits how fast an existing bill can grow each year (a low single-digit cap on primary residences), so a longtime owner's bill can drift far below what the current assessed value supports — and a sale can reset the math toward the full level for the new owner. The result: your bill may be meaningfully higher than the seller's, even with Nevada's famously moderate taxes. NV Ball Real Estate Group estimates the post-sale reality during escrow — the county assessor's numbers, not the seller's old bill — so closing day holds no surprises.

Estimate my real tax bill

How the cap works

Nevada's partial abatement limits annual increases on an existing owner's bill — a low cap for owner-occupied primaries, a higher one for other property. Years of capped growth can leave a longtime owner paying well under what current values support.

What a sale changes

The new owner's bill is built from the property's actual assessed math — and abatement status (primary versus rental, for instance) must be filed correctly with the county. The seller's old bill is history, not a forecast; we never let buyers budget on it.

How we estimate it

County assessor records plus the current tax math for your intended use — run during escrow, in writing. Nevada's taxes remain genuinely moderate (the California comparison is still lopsided); the point is budgeting on your number, not the seller's.

Straight answers

Why are my property taxes higher than what the seller paid?

Because the caps protected the SELLER'S bill, not yours — years of limited growth left it below what the assessment supports, and sale resets the math. Budget from the assessor's current numbers in escrow; the old bill is history, not a forecast.

What is Nevada's property tax cap?

The partial abatement: a low single-digit annual cap on primary residences, higher on other property — protection while you hold, not a discount that transfers. File your primary status correctly; we flag it at closing.

Are Nevada property taxes still low overall?

Still genuinely moderate — especially beside California's, with no income tax alongside. The surprise was never high taxes; it's budgeting on the seller's capped bill. We put your real number in writing during escrow.
