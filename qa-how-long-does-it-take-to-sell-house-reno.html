For sellers · The whole clock

How long does it take to sell a house in Reno?

The honest full clock, decision to funds: preparation runs one to three weeks (repairs, staging decisions, photography timed to your home's light), market time varies by segment (days for hot entry-level pockets, weeks for mid-market, months healthily for luxury and acreage — the live norms shift and we quote them in conversation), and escrow adds roughly thirty to forty-five days for financed buyers or as little as a week-plus for cash. Total for a typical financed mid-market sale: often six to ten weeks door to door, with the levers — pricing, condition, season — all yours to pull. The prep phase

One to three weeks, deliberately spent: the spend-or-skip walk, repairs at bid prices, cleaning and staging decisions, photography in your home's best light, and the paperwork head start (disclosures drafted, title's early look ordered on homes with history). Rushed prep costs more than it saves — the photos are forever.

The market phase

Segment sets the clock: hot entry pockets move in days, mid-market in weeks, luxury and acreage healthily in months — and pricing is the lever that dominates all others (the DOM translation explains the feedback loop). Live segment norms come in conversation; printed ones stale.

The escrow phase

Accepted offer to recorded deed: commonly thirty to forty-five days financed (inspection, appraisal, loan machinery), a week-plus possible for cash. The variables that stretch it — repair negotiations, appraisal timing, buyer-loan hiccups — are managed, not suffered. Then the wire.

Straight answers

How long does it take to sell a house in Reno from start to finish?

Typically six to ten weeks financed, door to door: 1–3 weeks prep, a segment-dependent market phase (days to months), and 30–45 days of escrow (cash: a week-plus). Pricing moves the clock most.

Can I sell faster if I need to?

Meaningfully — compressed prep, front-edge pricing, cash channels where the math justifies, fast escrow. Speed costs some net; we price the trade so you choose it.

What most commonly delays a sale?

The predictable three: overpricing (stretches market time), inspection surprises prep would've caught, and buyer-loan hiccups (prevented by vetting lenders at offer). All preventable — that's the hire.
