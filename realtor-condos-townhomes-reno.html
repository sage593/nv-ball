Condos & townhomes · Reno-Sparks

Buying a condo or townhome in Reno?

In condo-land, you're buying two things: the unit, and the association that runs the building. The second decides more than the first. NV Ball Real Estate Group guides condo and townhome purchases with the association's budget, reserve study, and rules read before you commit — plus the financing wrinkles some buildings carry flagged early, when they're still your decision.

The association audit

Budget, reserves, special-assessment history, rental caps, insurance structure. Nevada gives buyers a document-review window; we make it count.

Financing fine print

Some buildings complicate certain loan types. We check before you fall for a unit, not after a lender bounces it.

Who condos fit

First-time buyers, downsizers, and lock-and-leave travelers — often as part of a bigger plan we coordinate end to end.

Straight answers

Who helps buy condos and townhomes in Reno, NV?

We do — and in condo-land the HOA documents are half the purchase. We read the budget, reserves, and rules with you, and flag the financing wrinkles certain buildings carry, before you're committed.

What should condo buyers check that house buyers don't?

The association's health: budget, reserves, assessment history, owner-occupancy mix, rental rules. A gorgeous unit in a sick association is a sick purchase — we check the association first.

Are condos a good first purchase in Reno?

Often, yes — sensible entry price, less maintenance — if the association is healthy. We'll tell you plainly which buildings we'd buy in ourselves.
