For sellers · The rule nobody warns you about

Selling a house with a wood stove in Washoe County: the escrow rule

Here's the local rule that surprises sellers at the worst time: Washoe County's air-quality regulations reach into escrow — property sales involving wood-burning devices require inspection by a certified wood-burning-device inspector, and uncertified (pre-EPA-standard) stoves and inserts have long been required to come out at or before transfer, with narrow exemptions. Translation: that charming old stove can become a closing-week scramble if nobody checks it at listing time. NV Ball Real Estate Group flags the device question on day one of every listing that has one — here's how the process actually runs, with the caveat that the district's current code is always the final word.

Check my stove at listing, not closing

How the rule works

Washoe County's air-quality district regulates wood-burning devices through escrow: sales involving them call for a certified inspection, EPA-certified devices document and stay, and uncertified ones face the removal requirement (with limited exemptions — damage cases, certain cook stoves). The district's current regulation text and inspector list are the authority; we build the step into the listing timeline.

The seller's smart sequence

Identify the device at listing (the data plate tells the certification story), order the certified inspection early, and resolve removals or exemption paperwork before a buyer's escrow depends on it. Day-one handling costs a service call; week-four discovery costs a closing delay.

The buyer's side

Buying a home with a wood stove in Washoe County? The inspection paperwork belongs in your escrow file, and an inherited uncertified device is a question to resolve before closing — not a surprise to own after. Burn-code awareness (the county's red/yellow/green burn days) comes with the lifestyle; we brief it honestly.

Straight answers

Do I have to remove my wood stove to sell my house in Washoe County?

Uncertified (pre-EPA) device: very likely yes — the county's escrow-reaching rule requires removal at transfer, certified inspection included. Certified devices: document and stay. District code is final; check at listing, not closing week.

How do I know if my wood stove is EPA-certified?

The data plate usually tells: EPA labeling = certified; old and unlabeled = presumptively not. The district's certified inspector makes the escrow-grade call — we order it at listing.

Does this rule apply at Lake Tahoe and in the valleys too?

Washoe's rule covers Washoe — Incline and Crystal Bay included. Douglas, Carson, and Lyon run their own books. Per-address verification is the only honest method.
