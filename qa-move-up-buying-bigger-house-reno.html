For owners · The next-house question

Moving up to a bigger house in Reno: how the play actually works

The move-up is two transactions wearing one decision: your current home's equity funds the next one's down payment, which chains the timing — sell first and you're certain but temporarily housed; buy first and you're comfortable but carrying two; go contingent and you're safe but competing with an asterisk. The good news nobody leads with: move-up buyers control more than they think, because the equity is real, the local history is known (your house, your street, your comps), and the sequencing is designable. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs both ends of these chains weekly — here's the honest playbook.

Design my move-up

The three sequences

Sell-first: maximum certainty and negotiating strength, with a housing bridge (rent-back from your buyer is the classic — we negotiate them routinely). Buy-first: comfort at the cost of carrying two (bridge options and lender math decide feasibility). Contingent: safest and weakest in competition — workable in balanced segments, fragile in hot ones.

The equity audit first

Before touring anything: your current home's realistic net (live comps, not Zestimate nostalgia), your target segment's real pricing, and the lender's verdict on carrying scenarios. One afternoon of math converts the daydream into a plan — the valuation is free and obligates nothing.

The both-ends advantage

One team running both transactions means synchronized closings, rent-backs negotiated as part of your sale, and nobody's left guessing between two agents' calendars. The move-up chain's classic failure is coordination; hiring it away is the whole trick. The sell-first mechanics go deeper.

Straight answers

How do I buy a bigger house when I already own one in Reno?

Three sequences: sell-first (strong, rent-back bridges), buy-first (comfortable, lender permitting), contingent (safe, competition-weak). Start with the equity audit — real net, real target pricing, lender's verdict — and design from there.

Should I sell my house before buying the next one?

Usually, in competitive segments — sold-house buyers negotiate like cash, and rent-backs bridge the gap. Exception: strong finances + a rare-inventory target worth grabbing first.

Can I use my home's equity for the down payment before selling?

Mechanisms exist — bridge financing, pre-listing equity lines — with carrying costs that make them tools, not magic. Lender first; we sequence around what they bless.
