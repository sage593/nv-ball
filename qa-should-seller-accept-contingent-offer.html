For sellers · The offer with an asterisk

Should I accept an offer contingent on the buyer selling their home?

Sometimes — priced and protected: a home-sale contingency makes your sale depend on someone else's, which is real risk deserving real compensation (price, terms, or both) and real protection — the kick-out clause that lets you keep marketing and bump the contingent buyer if a cleaner offer arrives. The diligence that decides: the buyer's home — is it listed, priced sanely, in a selling segment? A contingent offer on a well-priced house in a moving market is a different animal than one on an unlisted dream. NV Ball Real Estate Group underwrites the chain before advising; here's the framework.

Underwrite the chain

The risk, priced

Contingency equals probability discount: your acceptance should harvest something — stronger price, better terms, tighter timelines — for the risk you're warehousing. A contingent offer at a premium can beat a clean offer at a discount; the grid decides, not the asterisk alone.

The kick-out clause

The standard protection: you keep marketing, and if a better offer lands, the contingent buyer gets a short window to drop their contingency or step aside. We write kick-outs into every contingent acceptance — warehousing risk without one is charity.

The chain diligence

Before advising, we underwrite their house: listed or theoretical? Priced against its comps or its memories? Sitting in a moving segment or a stale one? Their agent's track record closing chains? Twenty minutes of homework converts 'contingent' from a mystery into a probability — and probabilities can be priced.

Straight answers

Is it bad to accept a contingent offer on my house?

Not inherently — price it and protect it: compensation for the dependency, a kick-out clause (keep marketing, force their call on a better offer), and real diligence on THEIR house. Premium-contingent-protected can beat discounted-clean.

What is a kick-out clause?

Your protection: stay on market; a new offer gives the contingent buyer days to waive or walk. Waiting becomes optionality. Non-negotiable in our contingent acceptances.

How do I evaluate the buyer's house in the chain?

Like an underwriter: listed (not theoretical), comp-priced, moving segment, closing-capable agent. We pull answers first — chains are probabilities, not vibes.
