Buying · Protection

What is title insurance — and do I need it in Nevada?

Title insurance protects you from the past: liens nobody disclosed, recording errors, ownership claims that surface after you've bought. With a mortgage, a lender's policy is effectively mandatory; the owner's policy — a one-time premium at closing — is the one that protects your equity, for as long as you own the home. Nearly every Nevada buyer carries both, and should.

What it catches

Old liens, forged or mis-recorded documents, heirs nobody knew about. Rare — and ruinous without coverage. That asymmetry is the whole case.

The two policies

Lender's (protects the bank, required with a loan) and owner's (protects you, one-time cost, lasts your whole ownership). Different beneficiaries; don't skip yours.

Title + escrow together

In Nevada the title company usually runs escrow too: searching the record, holding funds neutrally, recording the deed. We work with the ones who answer their phones.

Straight answers

What is title insurance and do I need it in Nevada?

It protects against problems in the home's ownership history — old liens, recording errors, surprise claims. Lender's policy: effectively required with a mortgage. Owner's policy: a one-time premium that protects your equity for as long as you own. Nearly everyone carries both, sensibly.

What does a title company actually do in a Nevada sale?

Here, title and escrow usually travel together — the company searches the record, clears what must be cleared, holds everyone's money neutrally, and records the deed. The quiet machinery of closing day.

Who picks the title company?

Negotiable, often customary — and we steer toward escrow officers who answer their phones. On a tight closing, that one trait outranks everything.
