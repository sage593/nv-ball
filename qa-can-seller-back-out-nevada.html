For buyers & sellers · Cold feet

Can a seller back out of a home sale in Nevada?

Not easily, and that's the point of the contract: once signed, a seller's exits are few — a contingency written in their favor, the buyer's own breach, or a negotiated release the buyer agrees to. A seller who simply refuses to close is in breach, and the buyer's remedies are real: contract enforcement (specific performance — courts can compel the sale of unique property like real estate), damages, or a settlement that prices the exit. NV Ball Real Estate Group has sat on both sides of cold feet; here's the honest map of what actually happens.

Know the real exits

The legitimate exits

Contingencies written for the seller (rare but real — like finding replacement housing), buyer breach (missed deadlines, failed deposits), and mutual release — the buyer agrees to walk, often for consideration. Everything else is breach wearing a costume.

When a seller just refuses

Buyer remedies, in practice: specific performance (real estate is legally 'unique,' so courts can order the sale through), damages, or — most commonly — a negotiated settlement where the exit gets priced. Sellers who think listing remorse is a free walk-away discover otherwise; we counsel them before, not after.

The playbooks

Sellers with genuine remorse: talk early — negotiated releases are cheapest at the start and most expensive at the walkthrough. Buyers facing a wobbling seller: document everything, keep your own performance spotless, and decide your actual goal (the house or compensation) before choosing weapons. We've run both.

Straight answers

Can a seller back out of an accepted offer in Nevada?

Only narrowly: a seller-favoring contingency, buyer breach, or a negotiated release. Plain refusal = breach, and buyers can pursue specific performance (courts compel unique-property sales), damages, or a priced settlement. Remorse isn't a free exit.

What should I do if my seller is trying to back out?

Perform spotlessly and document everything; release nothing casually; pick your goal — house or compensation — then negotiate from strength. Most resolve once the seller's lawyer explains the math.

I'm the seller and I genuinely regret selling — what are my options?

Talk NOW — early releases are cheap; walkthrough-week refusal is expensive. Sometimes buyers walk graciously, sometimes for a price. Next time: seller contingencies get written in advance — we do that.
