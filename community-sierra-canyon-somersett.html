What living here is like

Sierra Canyon is the 55+ chapter of west Reno's Somersett master plan: Del Webb-built, largely single-level homes stepped along the canyon benches, with the Aspen Lodge running the social calendar — fitness, clubs, classes, the whole active-adult architecture — and Somersett's broader trail network and amenities layered on top. It's retirement living designed around motion rather than rocking chairs: pickleball mornings, trail afternoons, and Verdi's river country ten minutes west. For buyers comparing Nevada's active-adult options, this is the full-featured mountain-west version.

Who Sierra Canyon at Somersett fits

Strong fit: active 55+ buyers who want the clubs-classes-courts calendar with mountain-west scenery; single-level-living planners buying ahead of need; lock-and-leave travelers (the HOA handles the front-of-house); and California retirees pairing the lifestyle with Nevada's tax treatment.

Honest considerations: age-qualified rules are real — household eligibility deserves a check before falling in love; two amenity layers mean two fee layers — budget the full HOA picture; the canyon benches are genuinely higher and snowier than the valley floor by a note or two; and west-Reno wind has opinions some afternoons.

Sierra Canyon at Somersett FAQ

What is Sierra Canyon at Somersett?

Del Webb's 55+ village inside Somersett: single-level homes, the Aspen Lodge's full calendar, Somersett's trails layered on top — active-adult living, mountain-west edition.

What are the rules for living in a 55+ community?

Federal 55+ framework: typically one resident 55+, with community-specific rules on younger members and guests. We check your household against the live governing docs before hearts get involved.

Sierra Canyon or a regular neighborhood for retirement?

The fork: ready-made calendar, single-level design, age-peer community — versus flexibility and often more house elsewhere. Structure-lovers thrive here; fierce independents sometimes don't. We ask the blunt questions first.
