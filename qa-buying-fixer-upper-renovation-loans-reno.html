Buying a fixer-upper in Reno: how renovation loans work

You can finance the house and the fixing in one loan: FHA's 203(k) and conventional renovation programs roll purchase plus rehab into a single mortgage, with the limited versions covering cosmetic-to-moderate work and the full versions reaching structural projects. The honest catches: paperwork and timelines run heavier than standard loans, contractors must play by the program's rules, and the project math has to be real before you offer. NV Ball Real Estate Group pairs fixer-hunting with lenders who actually close these — and tells you when a house is too far gone for the program you're using.

Scope my project honestly

The loan landscape

FHA 203(k) in limited and standard flavors; conventional renovation options for stronger files. Each has scope rules — cosmetic versus structural — and your lender's live quote decides which fits. We keep the house inside the chosen program's rules.

What the programs demand

Bids from acceptable contractors, timelines, inspections or consultants on bigger projects, and a property that still meets baseline safety standards. The paperwork is heavier — worth it when the house is right, and we'll say when it isn't.

The honest project math

Purchase plus real rehab bids plus contingency, against the finished value the comps support. When the math pencils, fixers are the best value entry in established neighborhoods like the University District; when it doesn't, we say so before you're committed.

Straight answers

Can I get a loan to buy and renovate a house in Reno?

Yes — one loan for house plus rehab: FHA 203(k) or conventional renovation programs. Limited = cosmetic-to-moderate; standard = structural, more oversight. Trade: heavier paperwork and program rules. First hire: a lender who actually closes these.

What won't a renovation loan cover?

Houses below the program's safety baseline, structural work on the limited products, and scope edges that vary by program. Rules move — lender quotes current ones; we keep the house inside them.

Is a fixer-upper actually a good deal in Reno?

When the math is real — purchase + honest bids + contingency vs. the finished value comps support. Done right: best value entry in established Reno. Done on optimism: the classic regret. Math first, offer second.
