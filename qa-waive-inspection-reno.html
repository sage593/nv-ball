Buying · Risk

Should I waive the inspection to win a house in Reno?

Almost never — and in most competitive situations you don't have to. An inspection costs a few hundred dollars and routinely surfaces issues worth thousands; waiving it buys negotiating optics at real risk. The smarter competitive toolkit: shorten the inspection window, offer an information-only inspection, or strengthen price and terms instead. We'll tell you plainly when a bidding war is pushing you somewhere we wouldn't go ourselves.

The honest math

A few hundred dollars of inspection against five figures of surprise. That trade doesn't improve because other buyers are reckless.

The middle paths

Shortened windows and information-only inspections give sellers speed and certainty while you keep the exit for anything major.

Where we draw lines

Older homes, wells and septic, elevation properties — some houses we simply won't let you waive on without saying so in writing. You're hiring judgment, and you'll get it.

Straight answers

Should I waive the home inspection to win a house in Reno?

Almost never — and you rarely have to. Shorten the window, or go information-only (you still inspect; you pre-agree not to nickel-and-dime repairs), or strengthen other terms. Full waiver is the last resort, and we'll say so out loud if a bidding war pushes there.

What does an information-only inspection mean?

You still inspect, you can still walk for something major, but you signal no repair nickel-and-diming. Most of the seller comfort, a fraction of the buyer risk.

Do new homes need inspections too?

Yes. Brand-new homes produce real defect lists — our new-construction buyers always inspect independently. More on buying new.
