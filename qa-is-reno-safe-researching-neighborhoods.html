For movers · The research method

Is Reno safe? How to actually research it — and why agents can't answer

Two honest things up front: first, fair-housing law means real estate agents don't characterize neighborhood safety — not as evasion, but because steering is illegal and your risk tolerance is yours; second, you can research this better than anyone can summarize it for you, with primary sources: the Reno and Sparks police departments and Washoe County Sheriff publish crime data and maps, city open-data portals carry incident-level records, and national tools aggregate the same feeds — all more current and more granular than any ranking article. The method that works: research at block level (city-wide labels are nearly useless — conditions change street by street everywhere), compare like with like (dense entertainment districts log more incidents than cul-de-sacs by their nature), and finish with the test no database replaces: walk the blocks you're considering, day and evening. Here's the full method.

Tour with your own eyes

The primary sources

Local law-enforcement published data and crime maps (Reno PD, Sparks PD, Washoe County Sheriff), municipal open-data portals with incident-level records, and the aggregator tools built on those feeds — current, granular, and free of a ranking article's incentives. Pull them yourself for any address you're considering; the hour spent beats every listicle written.

The method that gets it right

Block level, not city level (every city's label hides street-by-street variation), categories read separately (property and violent incidents tell different stories; entertainment districts log volume by their nature), trends over snapshots, and context for density — per-capita readings shift when a district's daytime population triples. Then the irreplaceable step: walk it, day and evening, and trust what you see.

What we can and can't do

Can't: characterize neighborhoods' safety — fair-housing law prohibits steering, and honest agents everywhere hold that line. Can: point you to the primary sources above, tour any block you choose at any hour, and answer the fit questions that are ours to answer — commute, lifestyle, budget, trade-offs. Your eyes plus real data beat our adjectives; that's the honest division of labor.

Straight answers

Is Reno a safe place to live?

A method, not an adjective: agents can't steer (fair-housing law), and block-level beats city labels anyway. Pull police and open-data sources, read trends by category at block level, then walk the blocks day and evening.

Why won't a realtor tell me if a neighborhood is safe?

Steering is illegal under fair-housing law — and the rule serves you: primary data plus your own walk beats any agent's adjective. We point to sources and tour any block, any hour.

What's the best website to check crime in an area?

Closest to the source: police-published data and open-data portals, then aggregators for convenience. Block-level, by category, over time — and walk it yourself.
