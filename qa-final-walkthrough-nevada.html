For buyers · The last look

What is the final walkthrough — and what if something's wrong?

The final walkthrough is your last look at the property before closing — typically within the day or two prior — and it has one job: confirming the home is in the condition the contract promised. Agreed repairs done, included items present, no new damage, systems functioning, sellers' belongings gone. It is not a second inspection or a renegotiation window; it's a verification. And when something IS wrong, the answer is escrow mechanics, not panic: holdbacks, credits, or a brief delay solve almost everything. NV Ball Real Estate Group walks every walkthrough with a checklist and a plan for the exceptions.

Walk it with a checklist

What we check

Agreed repairs (with receipts), everything the contract includes still present, systems running (water, HVAC, appliances), no moving-day damage, and the place vacant and broom-clean as promised. Twenty minutes, methodical, documented with photos.

What it isn't

Not a new inspection, not a cosmetics renegotiation, not the moment for new asks. Walkthrough drama usually comes from mismatched expectations — we set them at contract so the last look is boring, which is the goal.

When something's wrong

The toolkit: escrow holdbacks (money held until the fix), closing credits, or a short delay. Almost nothing discovered at walkthrough should kill a closing — it should price or pause it briefly. We've run every version; calm mechanics beat day-before panic.

Straight answers

What is the final walkthrough when buying a house?

The last look before closing: verifying the contract's promises — repairs done, inclusions present, systems working, no new damage, sellers gone. A verification, not a renegotiation. With a checklist: twenty boring minutes, as intended.

What happens if the walkthrough finds a problem?

Mechanics: holdback, credit, or brief delay — one of the three resolves nearly everything. You check the promises while you still hold the unclosed-escrow leverage; that's the point.

Can I skip the walkthrough?

Don't. It's the only check between the seller's moving truck and your ownership — and its occasional discoveries get expensive after recording. Remote? We run it on video, documented.
