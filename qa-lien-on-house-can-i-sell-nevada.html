For sellers · Title surprises

There's a lien on my house — can I still sell it in Nevada?

Almost always, yes: most liens don't block a sale — they get paid from your proceeds at closing, handled by the title company as routine escrow work. Mortgages, HELOCs, solar liens, tax liens, HOA balances, old judgments: the title search surfaces them, escrow prices them, closing clears them. The sales that stumble are the ones where liens surprise everyone in week three — which is why we pull a preliminary title look early on any sale with history. The rare hard cases (liens exceeding equity, disputed claims) have paths too; they just need the math faced first.

Face the title math early

Routine clears

Mortgages and HELOCs, recorded solar agreements, property-tax balances, HOA dues, most judgments — all standard payoffs from proceeds at closing. If equity covers them, the sale barely notices; the title company runs the mechanics.

The early look

On any home with history — long ownership, past disputes, solar contracts, estate situations — we get title eyes on the record early. Week-one knowledge is strategy; week-three discovery is drama. The preliminary look costs nothing and prevents most of it.

The hard cases, honestly

Liens exceeding equity need negotiation (creditors routinely compromise to enable a sale), disputed liens need an attorney, and IRS situations have their own process — all navigable, none improvable by waiting. We've run each; the move is always facing the math first.

Straight answers

Can I sell my house if it has a lien on it?

Almost always — liens get paid from proceeds at closing, routinely. Hard mode is liens exceeding equity or disputes — navigable too. Either way: surface everything EARLY with a preliminary title look; week-three discovery is the only real enemy.

How do I find out what liens are on my property?

County records hold them; a preliminary title search reads them professionally. We order it early on any sale with history — cheap insurance against expensive surprises.

What if the liens add up to more than my house is worth?

Negotiation, not abandonment — creditors routinely compromise to enable closings, and lender shortfalls have the short-sale path. Paperwork-heavy, entirely runnable, done here before.
