For sellers · Spend where it returns

Which improvements actually add value before selling?

The directional truths hold across markets and years: curb appeal and exterior refreshes punch far above their cost (buyers decide at the curb), paint is the highest-leverage dollar in the catalog, minor kitchen-and-bath updates beat major remodels on pure return every time, and the money pits are predictable — pools, premium over-personalization, and the full gut renovation whose cost the next owner won't repay. The local layer matters too: what Reno buyers in your segment reward differs from generic national lists. NV Ball Real Estate Group walks every pre-sale home room by room and gives a spend-this-skip-that verdict against your actual comps — here's the framework.

The reliable winners

Paint (interior where it counts, the front door always), landscaping cleanup and curb appeal, deep cleaning and decluttering (free and decisive), lighting and hardware refreshes, and targeted repairs the inspection would flag anyway — fixed at bid prices now versus concession prices later. Modest money, outsized photographs.

The minor-beats-major rule

Kitchen and bath: refresh beats remodel — counters, hardware, fixtures, and paint recover better than full guts, because buyers repay updates but not your renovation ambitions. The full remodel is for your enjoyment or a flipper's spreadsheet, not a pre-sale checklist.

The money pits

Pools (our honest take), over-personalized premium finishes, room conversions that erase bedrooms, and any project whose timeline delays your listing into a worse season. The test for every dollar: does it change the photos or the inspection? If neither, keep it.

Straight answers

What improvements add the most value when selling a house?

Durable truths: curb appeal and exteriors return best, paint is the top-leverage dollar, minor kitchen/bath beats major remodels, cleaning is free and decisive. Pits: pools, over-personalization, full guts. Your segment's verdict beats any list.

Should I remodel my kitchen before selling?

Almost never fully — refreshes recover; remodels mostly don't. Dated-but-clean, priced right, frequently nets more. Remodel for your living, not your closing.

How do I decide what's worth doing on my specific house?

Two tests: does it change the photos or the inspection? Then the room-by-room verdict against your actual comps — which we give free, before contractors.
