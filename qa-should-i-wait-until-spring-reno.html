For buyers & sellers · The calendar question

Buy or sell now, or wait until spring? The trade played straight

The calendar question both sides ask all winter has a symmetrical answer: spring brings volume — more listings for buyers to choose from, more buyers for sellers to compete over — while winter brings leverage: the buyers out in January are serious (nobody tours in the cold recreationally), the sellers listed in January have reasons, and both sides negotiate with fewer competing bidders at the table. Which wins depends on what you're optimizing: selection favors spring, negotiating position often favors winter, and life timelines overrule both (the right house in December beats the theoretical house in April; the job that starts in February doesn't wait for inventory). The one false move: waiting for the market to decide for you. Here's the two-sided breakdown — and the holiday-selling version covers its own season.

Decide with real data

The buyer's version

Winter: fewer listings, but serious sellers, thinner competition, and offers that get negotiated rather than outbid — the bidding-war playbook gathers dust in January. Spring: selection arrives, and so does everyone else. Optimizing for choice says wait; optimizing for leverage says shop now; the right house appearing overrules both.

The seller's version

Spring: the buyer pool peaks, and so does your competition — every fence-sitting neighbor lists the same April weekend. Winter: fewer buyers, but serious ones, and your home competes against a thin shelf (the well-presented winter listing owns its price band in a way June never allows). Motivated-buyer quality versus buyer-pool quantity; your pricing strategy picks the season's version.

The overrule clause

Life timelines outrank seasonal theory: the February job start, the school-year sequencing, the house that appears in December — all beat waiting for a statistically prettier month. And the market itself shifts year to year (we quote current conditions in conversation, not published generalities), so the decision deserves live data over calendar folklore. Both versions of the math, run on your actual situation, in one conversation.

Straight answers

Should I wait until spring to buy a house in Reno?

Spring = selection plus competition; winter = leverage with serious sellers and thin bidding. Choice favors spring, position favors winter, and the right house or a life deadline overrules both.

Is winter a bad time to sell a house?

Different, not worse: serious buyers, thin competition, your listing owning its band — versus spring's volume on both sides. Live data and your timeline pick.

When do most homes come on the market in Reno?

The standard curve: building through spring, peaking early summer, thinnest at the holidays — buyers track it too. Thin seasons trade selection for leverage; live conditions beat the general curve.
