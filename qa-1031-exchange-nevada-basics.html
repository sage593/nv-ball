Investing · Tax strategy

What is a 1031 exchange — in plain English?

It's the investor's deferral play: sell an investment property, roll the proceeds through a qualified intermediary into another investment property, and defer the capital-gains tax that a plain sale would trigger. The mechanics forgive nothing — 45 days to identify replacements, 180 to close, intermediary custody throughout — which is why successful exchanges are planned backwards from the clocks. We run the real estate side to that timeline; your CPA and intermediary own the tax side. General information here, not tax advice.

Straight answers

What is a 1031 exchange, in plain English?

Sell one investment property, roll into another, defer the capital-gains tax — through a qualified intermediary, with a 45-day identification deadline and a 180-day closing deadline that forgive nothing. We run the real estate to that clock; your CPA and intermediary run the tax.

Can I 1031 from a California rental into a Nevada one?

Yes — and it's a classic Bay Area-investor move into Reno's market. California keeps its claw-back reporting on CA-origin gains, which is exactly why the CPA plans this one with you.

What goes wrong in 1031s?

Clocks and candidates: 45 days is short, and thin backups force bad buys. The fix is having targets scouted before your sale closes — our actual job in the play.
