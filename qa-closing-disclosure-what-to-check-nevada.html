The Closing Disclosure: what to actually check before you sign

Three business days before closing, your lender must deliver the Closing Disclosure — the final version of your loan and the transaction's money, line by line. Most buyers skim it; the discipline is checking five things hard: the loan terms against what you were quoted (rate, payment, any balloon or prepayment penalty), cash-to-close against expectations, the credits you negotiated actually appearing, fee changes since the Loan Estimate, and the boring-but-vital spellings and payoffs. Discrepancies caught in the window fix cheaply; discovered at the table, they fix frantically. NV Ball Real Estate Group reviews every CD beside our clients — here's our checklist.

Review mine with me

The five hard checks

Loan terms (rate, payment, balloon, penalties — versus your lock), cash to close (versus the estimate trail), negotiated credits present (sellers' concessions have vanished before), fee movement since the Loan Estimate (some fees can't increase; we flag ones that did), and names, addresses, payoffs spelled exactly right.

The three-day window's point

It exists so errors fix calmly: a wrong credit, a mispriced fee, a missing payoff gets corrected by email in the window — or renegotiated in panic at the table. We read the CD the day it arrives, beside you, on purpose.

When to pause a closing

Rarely needed, never feared: material loan-term changes restart review periods by law, and genuine discrepancies justify the uncomfortable phone call. A closing delayed a day beats a mistake amortized over thirty years — we've made that call and defend it. Pair this with the wire-fraud protocol; closing week deserves both disciplines.

Straight answers

What is the Closing Disclosure and when do I get it?

The final money document — by law, three business days before closing. Check hard: terms vs. quotes, cash-to-close vs. expectations, your credits actually present, fee movement, exact spellings. The window exists for exactly this.

What if the Closing Disclosure doesn't match the Loan Estimate?

Line-dependent: some fees can't legally rise, others have bands, and material term changes restart the clock. Legitimate updates happen; illegitimate ones fix cheaply in-window. We compare line by line.

Can a closing be delayed over CD problems?

Yes — and occasionally it should be. Material changes restart the clock by law; real discrepancies justify the pause. Rare, but a day's delay beats a 30-year mistake.
