What living here is like

Hidden Valley is the east side's quiet classic: an enclave that grew up around its country club decades before master-planning arrived, where fairway homes and horse properties share the same desert-backed streets and the regional park delivers trailheads at the end of the block. It sits minutes from downtown yet feels tucked away — the name is accurate — and its established-era homes offer square footage and lot sizes the newer communities price far higher.

Who Hidden Valley fits

Strong fit: value-sharp buyers who know established east-side square footage beats new-build pricing; golfers wanting the classic-club life without foothill premiums; horse households inside city minutes; buyers who like a neighborhood with decades of settled identity.

Honest considerations: 1960s–80s housing stock makes the full inspection suite mandatory — systems, roofs, galvanized-era plumbing; the desert edge brings wind and the occasional range wildlife; club membership is its own separate decision; and east-side perception lags its reality — which is precisely the value opportunity.

Hidden Valley FAQ

Is Hidden Valley a good place to live?

Yes — it's the east side's quiet classic: club, trails, horse edges, and square footage the newer communities can't price-match. Bring the full inspection suite and it's one of Reno's smarter buys.

How's the country club?

The enclave's spine — classic course, classic club culture. Membership is its own decision with the operator; we brief current terms.

Why is it priced below the west-side equivalents?

Perception lag, mostly — the measurables (minutes, square footage, club) outrun the old east-side mutter. That gap is the buy.
