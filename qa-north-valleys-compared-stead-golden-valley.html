The master key · North Valleys edition

Stead, Golden Valley, Cold Springs, or Sun Valley? The north, compared

The valleys north of Reno are the metro's value frontier, and they're not interchangeable: Stead and Lemmon Valley run the volume value play (newer subdivisions, big-box services, the airport's industrial energy), Cold Springs sits furthest with the gentlest new-build pricing, Sun Valley is the established manufactured-and-starter heart closest to town, Golden Valley holds the semi-rural middle (half-acres and horses, ten minutes out), and Silver Knolls with the Red Rock corridor goes full acreage. One axis organizes everything: how much land versus how much commute versus how much house. Every community has an honest guide here; this is the sorting page.

Sort my north valley

The subdivision tier

Stead & Lemmon Valley: the volume value answer — newer builds, services arriving with the growth, TRIC-reasonable logistics. Cold Springs: the furthest-and-gentlest-priced new-build frontier. Choose these for maximum house per dollar with suburban shape.

The established-value tier

Sun Valley: closest to town, manufactured-and-starter stock, the metro's most attainable ownership — with honest infrastructure trade-offs we state plainly in its guide. Choose it when entry price is the whole brief.

The land tier

Golden Valley: the semi-rural middle — half-acre-plus, horses behind houses, ten minutes from services. Silver Knolls & Red Rock: true acreage, wells-and-septic life, the full commitment. Choose by your honest appetite for systems ownership — we test it before showing either.

Straight answers

Which North Valleys community is best?

By brief: Stead/Lemmon (volume value), Cold Springs (gentlest new-build pricing, longest drive), Sun Valley (most attainable entry), Golden Valley (semi-rural half-acres), Silver Knolls (true acreage). The axis: land vs. commute vs. house.

Are the North Valleys a good place to buy a first home?

The metro's true entry tier — Sun Valley and Stead especially, programs lowering the bar further. Homework: real-shift commute math, wind and winter a notch harder, per-community infrastructure flags.

How is the commute from the North Valleys?

395 is the spine, rush hour real: Sun Valley closest (10–15), Stead/Golden Valley 15–20 off-peak, Cold Springs +10. TRIC commuters bypass the city — quiet advantage.
