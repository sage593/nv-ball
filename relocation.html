The relocation guide

Moving from the Bay Area?
Start here.

A large share of our clients are making exactly this move. This is the guide we wish every one of them had read first — what actually changes, where people land, and how buying here really works.

Talk to a relocation agent

Why people make the move

Three reasons come up in nearly every first call. Taxes: Nevada has no state personal income tax — for high earners, that alone can justify the move, though residency rules have teeth and a CPA should bless your plan. Housing: your money generally goes further here than in the Bay Area for a comparable home, though exactly how much depends on which metro you're leaving and which community you're targeting. Lifestyle: Lake Tahoe is under an hour from Reno, ski resorts closer still, and Reno-Tahoe International keeps the Bay a short flight or a familiar drive away.

The honest trade-offs

We'd rather you hear these from us. Summers bring wildfire smoke some weeks. Winter driving at elevation is real. Reno's food-and-culture scene has grown fast but it is not San Francisco's. And if you keep California income sources, your tax picture is more complicated than "Nevada has no income tax." None of these stop most of our clients — but the happiest transplants are the ones who knew them going in.

Where transplants land

Remote workers & families

South Reno, Damonte Ranch, Somersett, Spanish Springs — newer construction, master-planned amenities, easy airport access.

Space and small-town pace

The Carson Valley — Gardnerville, Minden, and Genoa — ranch-style properties, Sierra views, and a slower rhythm 45 minutes south of Reno.

Mountain lifestyle

Lake Tahoe and Verdi — alpine living with its own rules: snow loads, STR regulations, and premium pricing.

Value and room to grow

Sparks, Dayton, Fernley, and Fallon — more home for the money, growing fast, and favorites for first purchases in Nevada.

What's different here

Buying in Nevada, for California buyers

Disclosures

Nevada has its own regime — including the Seller's Real Property Disclosure — and rural quirks like open range and well/septic disclosures you won't have seen in the Bay.

HOAs & CICs

Many newer communities are common-interest communities. We read the HOA documents with you before you commit, not after.

Terrain & climate

Elevation, snow, and wildfire zones affect insurance, inspections, and which homes we'd steer you toward. Local knowledge is the product here.

The mechanics — escrow, inspection, appraisal, closing — will feel familiar. The judgment calls are where a local team earns its keep. See our full buying process.

Common questions

Asked on nearly every relocation call

Can we look at homes before we've moved?

Yes — most of our relocation clients start with video tours and a scouting weekend. We'll build a shortlist around your priorities so a two-day visit does the work of two months.

Should we rent first or buy right away?

It depends on how well you know where you want to be. If you've only seen Reno once, renting for six months in your target area is often money well spent. If you know your community, buying sooner avoids paying twice in moving costs. We'll tell you honestly which camp you're in.

How does the school picture compare?

Washoe County, Douglas County, and the surrounding districts each have their own character, and the honest answer varies by school, not district. We'll connect you with current local parents rather than quoting a ranking site.

What about keeping our Bay Area jobs?

Very common. Many clients work remotely with periodic trips back — Reno-Tahoe International runs frequent Bay Area flights, and the drive to Sacramento and the Bay is familiar to every transplant within a year.
