Selling your parents' house: the transition sale, handled gently

This sale is different and deserves to be treated differently: a home held for decades (with the deferred maintenance and vintage systems that implies), parents moving to assisted living or closer to family (often on a timeline set by health, not by markets), adult children coordinating from other cities, and a house full of a lifetime's belongings — all before the first real-estate decision gets made. NV Ball Real Estate Group handles senior-transition sales with the extras built in: the authority questions sorted first (who can legally sign — owner, trustee, or power of attorney — determines everything downstream), estate-sale and clear-out logistics coordinated, as-is-versus-prepare math run honestly against the family's bandwidth, and communication built for siblings in three time zones. If you're moving parents toward you instead, that guide pairs with this one. Here's how it works.

Help our family with this

Authority first

Who signs determines everything: the owner directly (capacity matters — sensitive, and real), a trustee under a living trust (the trust-sale guide covers it), or an agent under power of attorney (the document's scope gets read before escrow does; title companies verify, so we verify first). Sorted in week one, these questions never become week-six crises — and where the owner has passed, the probate path is its own page.

The logistics nobody warns you about

Decades of belongings: estate-sale services, donation runs, family-keepsake sessions scheduled before clear-out crews arrive — we coordinate the vendor sequence so out-of-town siblings aren't flying in for every step. The house itself: vintage systems and deferred maintenance priced honestly in the as-is-versus-prepare decision, where the right answer is the family's bandwidth, not a renovation show's.

Communication built for families

Every sibling on the same updates (decisions documented, not relayed through one exhausted coordinator), the timeline shaped around the move that matters (the parent's transition, not the listing calendar), and patience as policy — these sales carry grief and logistics in equal measure, and twenty consecutive 5-star reviews include families who met us exactly here.

Straight answers

How do I sell my parents' house for them?

Authority first: owner, trustee, or POA (verified before escrow). Then keepsakes, clear-out coordination, honest as-is-versus-prepare math, and a timeline shaped around the parent's move — not the listing calendar.

Do I need power of attorney to sell my parents' home?

Only if they won't sign themselves. Trusts use trustees; POAs need real-property scope that title will verify. No valid path + lost capacity = attorney first, honestly.

Should we fix up the house or sell it as-is?

Run both versions: light prep usually pays, major renovation of a dated home rarely does — and family bandwidth is a real cost. Clean-out plus refresh, honestly priced, wins often.
