Pre-qualified vs. pre-approved: what's the real difference?

Pre-qualification is an estimate built from what you tell a lender — nothing verified, useful for early budgeting. Pre-approval is the verified version: credit pulled, income and assets documented, a lendable number in writing. Sellers and listing agents read the difference instantly, which is why serious offers get written on pre-approvals and 'pre-qualified' buyers lose houses to equally priced competition. It's the single highest-leverage first step in any purchase — and it's free.

Straight answers

What's the difference between pre-qualified and pre-approved?

Pre-qualified = an estimate from what you told a lender. Pre-approved = they verified credit, income, and assets and put a number in writing. Sellers read the second as real and the first as a maybe — so we write offers on pre-approvals, full stop.

How long does pre-approval take and last?

Days, once your documents are in; letters typically live 60–90 days and refresh easily. Gather documents before you shop — it's the only slow step.

Does pre-approval hurt my credit?

Modestly and temporarily — and rate-shopping pulls within the scoring window count as one inquiry, so comparing lenders doesn't stack it. Shop freely.
