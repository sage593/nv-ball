Selling a house with solar panels in Nevada

The one question that decides everything: owned or leased? Owned systems are part of the house and usually help the sale. Leased systems belong to the solar company — the buyer must qualify to assume the lease or you buy it out, and sorting that before listing is the difference between a selling point and a week-three crisis. NV Ball Real Estate Group starts every solar listing with the paperwork, then builds the pricing and disclosure plan around what it says.

Sort out my solar sale

Leased: the transfer path

Assumption means the buyer qualifies with the solar company; buyout means a payoff number you want in writing early. Both take lead time — we start that clock before the sign goes up.

Owned: the selling point

Production history and warranty documents turn 'has solar' into a priced advantage. We package them the way buyers and appraisers actually weigh them.

Disclosure, done right

Nevada's seller disclosure framework means the system's ownership status goes in writing from day one — our disclosure guide covers the wider form, and late surprises stay off your escrow.

Straight answers

Can I sell my house with leased solar panels in Nevada?

Yes — but the lease writes the playbook. Leased panels belong to the solar company: the buyer assumes the lease (and must qualify) or you buy it out, and that decision belongs before listing, not mid-escrow. Owned systems are the opposite: part of the house, usually a selling point.

Do solar panels add value to a home in Reno?

Owned: generally yes — Nevada sun is real and buyers get the math on lower bills. Leased: murkier — appraisers treat them as the company's property, and the payment is a negotiation item, not automatic value. We'll tell you which side of that line you're on.

What should I do before listing a solar home?

Three calls: the solar company (transfer and buyout terms, in writing — they're slow), your paperwork drawer (contract, production records, warranty), and us (pricing and disclosure plan). The system's status goes in the disclosures — late surprises here are how deals die expensively.
