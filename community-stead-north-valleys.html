What living here is like

Stead and the North Valleys do Reno's unglamorous heavy lifting: logistics parks and the historic airfield anchoring jobs, newer subdivisions delivering the metro's most attainable square footage, and US-395 putting Midtown fifteen minutes south when wanted. The plateau's personality is practical — garages that work for a living, big weather skies, the air-race heritage overhead — and its value math keeps first-time buyers arriving every season the market tightens elsewhere.

Who Stead & the North Valleys fits

Strong fit: first-time buyers chasing attainable square footage inside city limits; logistics-and-airfield-corridor workers minimizing commutes; value investors reading the workforce-rental logic; big-sky temperaments who like their valleys open.

Honest considerations: winters run colder and whiter than downtown's — plan accordingly; pockets vary meaningfully (Silver Lake, Lemmon Valley, Stead proper each read differently — local knowledge earns its keep); services are improving but still catching the rooftops; and historic flood-mapping in specific low spots is checkable due diligence we run routinely.

Stead & the North Valleys FAQ

Is Stead a good place to live?

For attainable square footage with jobs next door — yes, it's the metro's workhorse answer. Winter means it up here, and pockets differ: that's what we're for.

What's the airport story?

The airfield that hosted the air races for decades — the district's landmark and occasional soundtrack. Heritage overhead, literally.

Which pockets need extra homework?

Lemmon Valley's low spots get the flood-map check as routine, and each pocket reads its own way. Street-level knowledge is the product here.
