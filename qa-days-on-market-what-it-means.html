For buyers & sellers · The listing clock

What does days on market actually tell you?

DOM is a signal that needs a denominator: 60 days means nothing until you know the segment's norm — entry-level homes in hot pockets move in days while luxury and acreage properties healthily take months, so the real read is this listing's clock against its segment's typical clock. For buyers, long-relative DOM usually means price (sometimes condition or access), and it means leverage. For sellers, the clock is feedback arriving daily — and the stigma question ('what's wrong with it?') is managed by responding to the feedback, not waiting it out. NV Ball Real Estate Group reads the clock segment-by-segment — here's the translation.

Translate the clock

The denominator rule

Every segment has its own normal: entry-level in demand pockets moves in days; mid-market in weeks; luxury, acreage, and unique properties in months, healthily. DOM only speaks when divided by its segment's norm — we quote those norms live, since printed ones stale.

The buyer's read

Long-relative DOM most often means price, occasionally condition, access problems, or bad photos hiding a good house (the quiet opportunity category). It always means leverage: less competition, more negotiability, sellers hearing the market's verdict daily. The question isn't 'what's wrong' — it's 'what's mispriced, and is it fixable or discountable?'

The seller's management

The clock is feedback: showings without offers point at price; no showings point at price or presentation; and the response window matters — adjustments in the first weeks read as responsive, while month-three cuts read as capitulation. The relaunch playbook exists for clocks that ran too long.

Straight answers

Is a house that's been on the market a long time a red flag?

Only against its segment's norm — luxury and acreage healthily take months; a starter past its pocket's norm usually means price (sometimes condition, access, or bad photos). Either way: buyer leverage.

What's a normal number of days on market in Reno?

Segment- and season-dependent: days for hot entry-level, weeks mid-market, months up-tier. Current norms quoted live; printed ones stale.

My listing's clock is running — when should I adjust?

Early and decisive beats late and grudging — the first weeks' feedback IS the market's pricing. Pre-set checkpoints at listing; skip the month-three capitulation.
