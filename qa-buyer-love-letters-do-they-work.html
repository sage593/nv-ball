For buyers · The offer extra

Buyer love letters: do they work, and should you write one?

The straight answer: sometimes they've swayed sellers, and the practice is dying anyway — because love letters carry a fair-housing problem both sides' professionals increasingly refuse to touch. The letters routinely reveal protected characteristics (family status, religion, national origin — the photo of your family in front of the oak tree says plenty), which exposes sellers to discrimination claims if they pick or pass on buyers after reading them; many listing agents now decline to deliver them, some states have tried restricting them, and the trend runs one direction. The better news: everything a love letter tries to do, a stronger offer does cleanly — terms are the love language sellers act on. NV Ball Real Estate Group builds offers that compete on substance; here's the full picture.

Compete on substance

Why the practice is fading

The fair-housing mechanics: letters reveal protected characteristics, and a seller who chooses between buyers after reading them invites claims that the choice ran on protected grounds — so listing agents increasingly refuse delivery to protect their clients, and buyer agents increasingly advise against writing. It's not cynicism; it's everyone's exposure, including the seller you're courting.

What actually moves sellers

Terms: clean contingency structure, honest timelines matched to the seller's needs (the rent-back that solves their next-house problem beats any paragraph about your garden plans), earnest money that signals commitment, lender credibility (a local underwriter's pre-approval reads differently than an internet pre-qual), and certainty signals like appraisal strategy deployed deliberately. The bidding-war playbook is all substance; sellers act on it.

If you still want the human touch

The agent-to-agent channel carries it cleanly: your agent conveying 'these buyers are solid, flexible on close date, and genuinely committed' transmits warmth without documents revealing protected characteristics — professional reputation does what the letter wanted to. That conversation is part of every offer we present.

Straight answers

Do love letters to home sellers work?

Occasionally — but the practice is dying: letters reveal protected characteristics and create seller liability, so many listing agents refuse them. Terms are the love language sellers act on; agent-to-agent conversation carries the warmth cleanly.

Why would a listing agent refuse my love letter?

Seller protection: choosing buyers after reading letters that reveal protected characteristics invites discrimination claims. Refusal is risk management, not rudeness — terms always get delivered.

What should I do instead of a love letter?

Substance: certainty-built contingencies, seller-matched dates (ask what they need), committed earnest money, credible local pre-approval, and your agent's professional case made agent-to-agent.
