What living here is like

Somersett lives like a quiet resort: morning miles on the community's own trail network, golf at the country club or the community's short course, and evenings when the whole western sky performs. The villages each have their own character — and their own sub-HOA — stacked across the foothills, so views, wind, and winter vary meaningfully street by street. That's not a flaw; it's the terrain's personality, and knowing it is the local advantage.

Who Somersett fits

Strong fit: view seekers and golfers; buyers who want master-planned polish with real topography; remote workers who trade commute minutes for trail minutes; move-up buyers who've been circling west Reno for years.

Honest considerations: the HOA stack (master plus village) deserves a careful read — dues and rules differ by village; elevation means more weather than the valley floor, including wind with opinions; downtown is a drive, not a stroll; and golf-adjacent lots carry premiums worth pricing honestly.

Somersett FAQ

Is Somersett a good place to live?

If views, trails, and golf rank high — yes, it's west Reno's flagship for a reason. Read the two-layer HOA carefully and respect the elevation's weather, and it's hard to beat.

What is Del Webb at Sierra Canyon?

Somersett's 55+ village — single-level homes, its own lodge, same views. One of the region's most-asked-about active-adult addresses.

How windy is it really?

Real enough to mention — and very street-by-street. We'll tell you at the showing, not after closing.
