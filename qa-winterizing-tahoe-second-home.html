Winterizing a Tahoe second home: the checklist that prevents disasters

The disaster has one shape: an unattended home, a hard freeze, a burst pipe, and weeks of running water before anyone notices. Prevention is a ritual, not a mystery — water managed (full shutdown and drain-down for long absences, or heat-plus-monitoring for in-and-out winters), heat strategy chosen deliberately, snow management arranged (roofs and driveways both), and someone or something watching: a caretaker, a neighbor, or the leak-and-temperature sensors that have made remote monitoring cheap. NV Ball Real Estate Group hands this checklist to every second-home buyer at closing — here's the whole thing.

Set up my absence plan

The water decision

Long absences: shut off the main, drain the system, protect traps — the full shutdown that makes freezes irrelevant. In-and-out winters: heat held at safe minimums plus leak sensors and a shutoff you can reach remotely. The wrong answer is the middle: water on, nobody watching.

Heat, snow, and the roof

Heat strategy set deliberately (safe minimums cost less than repairs); snow removal arranged before the first storm (driveway contracts fill early at the lake); roof-load and ice-dam awareness for big-winter years. Altitude homes — Kingsbury, Incline's upper streets — run the intense version of all three.

The watching layer

Modern monitoring is cheap insurance: leak sensors, temperature alerts, smart shutoffs, cameras — plus the human layer (caretaker service or a trusted neighbor) for what sensors miss. Insurance policies increasingly care about occupancy and monitoring too; know your policy's absence rules before testing them.

Straight answers

How do I winterize a second home at Tahoe?

Decide water (drain-down for long absences; heat + sensors for in-and-out), hold safe heat, contract snow removal early, and add a watching layer — sensors, shutoffs, a human. The burst-pipe disaster is entirely preventable.

Should I leave the heat on in an empty mountain house?

Water on → heat at safe minimums + monitoring. Gone all season → drain everything; freezes can't burst empty pipes. The danger is the middle: water on, heat gambling, nobody watching.

Does insurance care how I manage absences?

Increasingly yes — occupancy patterns and vacancy clauses matter, and precautions are expected. Know the rules before a claim tests them; document the ritual.
