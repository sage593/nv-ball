For buyers · The frozen weeks

What not to do before closing: the don't-touch-anything list

Your approval is a snapshot, and underwriting photographs you again right before closing — so the weeks between contract and keys run on one rule: change nothing the snapshot captured. The canon: no new credit (cards, cars, furniture-on-financing — the classic deal-killer bought for the new house), no job changes without your lender in the loop first, no large untraceable deposits (lenders won't count money without a paper trail — gifts have their documented path), no missed payments, no account closures, and no co-signing anyone's anything. The escape valve for all of it: the call-first rule — lenders can plan around what they know and die on surprises. NV Ball Real Estate Group briefs this at contract; here's the list on the refrigerator.

Freeze my snapshot properly

The credit freeze

No new accounts, no big balances, no closures, no co-signing — underwriting re-pulls before closing, and one new car payment can reprice your ratios into denial. The furniture for the new house waits for the keys; everyone's cautionary tale bought it early.

The income-and-money stillness

Job changes mid-escrow need the lender's blessing first (some transitions survive fine — announced; few survive as surprises), and deposits need provenance: large untraceable cash stalls files for weeks of seasoning. Money moves planned at pre-approval glide; improvised ones explain themselves to underwriters.

The call-first rule

Life happens mid-escrow — bonuses, job offers, family gifts, emergencies — and nearly all of it survives when the lender plans for it: the call before the move keeps options open; the discovery after burns them. Our closing brief ends with the lender's number highlighted; use it recreationally.

Straight answers

What should I avoid doing before my house closes?

The canon: no new credit, no un-looped job changes, no mystery deposits, no missed payments, no closures, no co-signing. You get re-photographed before closing — change nothing, and call the lender BEFORE life moves.

Can I buy furniture or appliances before closing?

Financed: no — the classic tale. Planned cash: generally fine within reason. Keys first; the sectional waits.

What happens if my job changes during escrow?

Sequence decides: announced first, many changes survive; discovered later, the same change sinks files. The move's allowed; the surprise isn't.
