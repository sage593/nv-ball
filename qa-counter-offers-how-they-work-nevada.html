For buyers & sellers · The volley

How do counter offers work in Nevada?

A counter offer rejects the offer it answers and proposes new terms — legally, the original is dead the moment you counter, which is the fact that disciplines everything else. From there it's a volley: each side may counter, accept, or walk, nothing binds until one side accepts in writing unchanged, and everything is in play — price, credits, timelines, inclusions, contingencies, rent-backs — not just the number. The strategy mistakes repeat forever: sellers countering a strong offer into oblivion over trivial sums; buyers bidding against themselves; both sides forgetting that terms often move more value than price. The mechanics that matter

Countering kills the original offer — you can't fall back to it if the buyer walks (a fact that has cost many sellers their best offer). Nothing binds until written acceptance of unchanged terms; expiration times are real; and verbal agreements are conversation, not contract.

Beyond the price line

Credits and buydowns, close-of-escrow dates, rent-backs, inclusions, contingency windows, as-is postures — each is currency. The best counters trade cheap-to-give for expensive-to-get; the worst fight over the last thousand while ignoring terms worth ten.

The classic mistakes

Sellers: countering a clean, strong offer over trivial sums — the counter that risks everything to gain little. Buyers: bidding against yourself when no counter has arrived, or treating the volley as personal. Both: negotiating without knowing the leverage picture — which is the part we actually bring.

Straight answers

How does a counter offer work?

A counter kills the offer it answers — no falling back if they walk. The volley runs until someone accepts unchanged terms in writing. And everything's in play: price, credits, dates, inclusions, contingencies.

Should a seller counter every offer?

Usually — counters keep conversations alive. The exception: a strong clean offer, where countering over pennies risks everything. Knowing which you're holding is the judgment you hire.

How many rounds of counters are normal?

One or two rounds settle most deals. Marathons signal unbridgeable gaps or pride with paperwork — we'll tell you which, honestly.
