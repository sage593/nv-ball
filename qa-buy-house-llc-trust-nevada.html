For buyers · Structure questions

Should I buy a Nevada home in an LLC or trust?

It depends on what you're actually buying: privacy, liability separation, or estate planning — each points to different structures with real trade-offs. The honest outline: LLCs suit investment property (liability separation) but complicate owner-occupied financing; trusts are the standard estate-planning vehicle and generally play fine with home purchases; and privacy via entity ownership is partially real, partially oversold. The structure decision belongs to your attorney and CPA. NV Ball Real Estate Group executes whichever they bless — and we've closed plenty of each.

Structure my purchase right

LLCs: the investor's tool

Liability separation for rentals is the legitimate use. The trade-offs: conventional owner-occupied financing generally won't lend to an LLC, lender menus narrow, and the formalities must actually be maintained to mean anything. Attorney territory; we execute.

Trusts: the estate standard

Revocable living trusts are how many households hold homes for estate-planning reasons, and purchases and refis generally accommodate them. Common, boring, effective — your estate attorney structures it.

Privacy, honestly

Entity ownership keeps your name off some public records, not all trails, and Nevada's recording system is what it is. For luxury buyers wanting discretion, the entity is one tool among several — quiet process matters as much as paperwork.

Straight answers

Can I buy a house in an LLC in Nevada?

Yes — standard for investment property; liability separation is the point. Trade-offs: owner-occupied conventional financing generally won't do it, lender menus narrow, and the formalities must be real. Attorney designs; we execute.

Does an LLC or trust hide who owns a home?

Partially — your name leaves some public records, but it's privacy, not invisibility. One tool among several; a genuinely quiet process is usually worth as much.

Should my family home be in a trust?

Common and routine — revocable trusts hold many Nevada family homes. Whether yours should: estate attorney's call. We make the transaction match the plan.
