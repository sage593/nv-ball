Using gift money for your down payment: the rules that matter

Family gift funds power a huge share of first purchases, and lenders welcome them — documented their way: a gift letter (donor, amount, relationship, and the magic words — no repayment expected), a paper trail of the transfer (large cash appearing mysteriously in your account is the classic stall), and program rules about who may give and how much of the down payment gifts may cover (generous for primary residences under most programs; your lender quotes the current specifics). Done right, it's routine; improvised, it's the week-three underwriting scramble. NV Ball Real Estate Group flags the gift conversation at pre-approval — here's the clean version.

Paper the gift properly

The gift letter

The lender's form, signed by the donor: who's giving, how much, the relationship, and that repayment isn't expected (a disguised loan changes your debt math and misstating it is fraud, plainly). Takes ten minutes; underwriting won't move without it.

The paper trail

Money moves traceably: donor's account to yours (or straight to escrow — often cleanest), with statements showing the source. Mattress cash and mystery deposits stall files for weeks of seasoning; the transfer's boring documentation is the whole game. Plan the movement before the house hunt, not during escrow.

The program rules

Who may give (family broadly; rules vary at the edges), how much of the down payment gifts may cover (often all of it on primary-residence programs), and how gifts interact with assistance programs — current specifics are your lender's to quote, and the pre-approval conversation is where the gift gets engineered in cleanly.

Straight answers

Can my parents give me money for a down payment?

Yes — routine, documented properly: gift letter (no repayment expected), traceable transfer with source statements, program rules quoted by your lender. Plan the money's movement at pre-approval; mystery deposits stall files.

Does gift money have tax consequences?

Recipient: generally none. Donor: annual-exclusion and filing questions for their CPA (figures change yearly). Lender documentation and tax advice are separate tracks — run both.

Can the gift be a loan we plan to repay quietly?

No — plainly. The letter certifies no repayment; misstating it is fraud. Real family loans can sometimes be structured honestly — say so and do that instead.
