For international buyers · The open door

Can foreigners buy property in Nevada? Yes — here's how it works

The clean answer first: the United States places no citizenship requirement on owning real estate — visa holders, permanent residents, and buyers living abroad all purchase Nevada property legally and routinely. The practical layers are where guidance earns its keep: financing (cash is simplest; foreign-national loan programs exist with larger down payments; established-credit visa holders often qualify conventionally), identification and tax plumbing (ITINs where SSNs don't exist), ownership structure questions worth attorney time, and the resale-side FIRPTA withholding every foreign seller should know about before buying. NV Ball Real Estate Group works with international and visa-holder buyers — here's the honest map.

Map my purchase from anywhere

Who buys, and how often

H-1B and other visa holders building lives here, foreign investors, parents buying near UNR students, and returning expats — all legal, all routine. No citizenship requirement exists for ownership; the differences are machinery, not permission.

The financing tiers

Cash: simplest, same as anyone (with source-of-funds documentation done properly). Established visa holders with US credit: often conventional programs on normal terms. Foreign nationals without US credit: specialized programs — larger down payments, rate premiums, fewer lenders — that genuinely work. We route buyers to the lenders who actually close each tier.

The tax plumbing

ITINs stand in where SSNs don't exist; ownership structures (individual, LLC, trust) deserve attorney counsel against estate and treaty questions; and FIRPTA — the withholding rule on foreign sellers at resale — is the one to learn before buying, not discover at selling. Your cross-border CPA owns specifics; we build the team around the purchase.

Straight answers

Can a non-US citizen buy a house in Nevada?

Yes — no citizenship requirement. Visa holders, residents, and overseas buyers purchase routinely. The machinery: financing tiers by credit history, ITINs where needed, and FIRPTA (the foreign-seller withholding) learned before buying, not at selling.

Can someone on an H-1B or work visa get a mortgage?

Commonly yes — US income and credit usually mean conventional terms; lenders vary in visa appetite (we route accordingly). No credit history yet: foreign-national programs — workable, pricier.

What is FIRPTA and should it scare me off?

No — withholding, not penalty: escrow withholds a slice of a foreign seller's price toward taxes, reconciled by filing. It shapes exit cash timing, not your rights. Routine with a cross-border CPA.
