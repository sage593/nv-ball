Well and septic inspections: the rural diligence done properly

Every rural guide on this site says 'we run the well-and-septic file' — this page is the file: the well inspection covers production (flow testing against your household's reality), water quality (the standard panel: bacteria, nitrates, and the local-context additions like arsenic that Nevada groundwater sometimes carries), and equipment condition (pump age, pressure tank, casing); the septic side covers tank condition and pumping history, leach-field function, and capacity against your actual household. Both are specialist inspections beyond the general inspector's scope, both are contingency-protected homework, and both convert the rural dream's biggest unknowns into priced facts. Here's what each test means.

Run my systems file

The well file

Flow/production test (gallons per minute against household demand — gardens and animals raise the bar), the water-quality panel (bacteria and nitrates standard; arsenic and other locals worth adding in known areas — Nevada groundwater varies by basin), equipment read (pump age and type, pressure tank, casing condition), and the paperwork (well log, any rights context per the water-rights guide).

The septic file

Tank inspection (condition, baffles, last pumping — history requested from sellers), leach-field evaluation (the expensive component's function), capacity-versus-household math, and the county records check (permitted size and any repair history). Failing fields are five-figure conversations — found in escrow they negotiate; found after they're yours.

The results playbook

Good results: file them and relax — you now know your systems. Marginal results: price them (pump nearing retirement, tank due for service — maintenance items with numbers). Bad results: negotiate seriously or walk protected — contingencies exist for exactly the failing leach field. Every outcome beats the untested version.

Straight answers

What inspections do I need when buying a house with a well and septic?

The specialist pair: well (flow vs. your demand, quality panel + local additions like arsenic, equipment read) and septic (tank + history, leach-field function, capacity, records). Contingency-protected priced facts.

What should a well water test include in Nevada?

Standard (bacteria, nitrates) + Nevada context: arsenic nearly always worth adding, area extras by geology. Treatment handles most findings — test first.

What if the septic inspection finds problems?

By tier: service items price in; failing fields (five figures) negotiate with bids or exit protected. The point is having this talk BEFORE closing.
