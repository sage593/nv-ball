Exchanging your California rental into Nevada: the clawback-honest guide

The move is popular for real reasons — trading California landlording (tenant-law intensity, the tax drag on income) for Nevada property under landlord-workable law with no state tax on the rental income — and it carries homework most articles skip: California doesn't forget deferred gain. Exchange a California rental into Nevada property and the FTB requires Form 3840 filed with California annually, tracking the California-source deferred gain until it's recognized — and when you eventually sell the Nevada property in a taxable sale, California claims its tax on that deferred slice. The exchange still pencils brilliantly for most (future appreciation and income escape California entirely); it just pencils honestly with the clawback in the model. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs the Nevada leg regularly — here's the straight version.

Model the exchange honestly

Why investors make the move

The go-forward math: Nevada rental income faces no state income tax (California's drag ends for new earnings), Nevada appreciation accrues outside California's reach, landlord-tenant law here runs workable rather than adversarial, and the properties — our investor lanes — serve the strategy. The past gain carries homework; the future belongs to Nevada.

The clawback, plainly

California-source deferred gain stays California's claim: Form 3840 files annually with the FTB (moving yourself to Nevada doesn't end the duty), subsequent exchanges keep the tracking alive, and a future taxable sale of the Nevada property triggers California's tax on the deferred slice. Skipping the filing invites penalties and FTB attention — the honest model includes the claim; your CPA runs the form.

Running the exchange right

The standard 1031 machinery applies — qualified intermediary, 45-day identification, 180-day close (our exchange practice) — with the California layer added: the 3840 duty understood before closing, exit scenarios modeled with the clawback (hold long, exchange again, or the step-up endgame estates sometimes reach — all CPA architecture). Eyes-open beats surprised, every time.

Straight answers

Can I 1031 exchange my California rental into a Nevada property?

Yes — standard 1031, popular move, with homework: Form 3840 files with California annually (even after you move), and the deferred CA gain gets clawed back at a future taxable sale. Future income and growth escape CA; the past gain stays in the model.

Does moving to Nevada myself erase California's claim on the deferred gain?

No — the claim follows the gain's source, not your address. New earnings escape; the deferred slice doesn't. Both-states CPAs model the endgames.

Is the exchange still worth doing with the clawback?

Usually — the clawback is yesterday's gain, eventually; everything forward escapes now. Regret comes from skipped models or filings; run both.
