Living in Reno, Nevada

The Biggest Little City has outgrown its nickname. Between the Sierra Nevada foothills and the Truckee River, Reno mixes a walkable urban core, established mid-century neighborhoods, and fast-growing master-planned communities — with no state income tax and Lake Tahoe forty-five minutes away.

Who Reno fits

Reno draws three kinds of movers, and we work with all of them: California transplants trading commute time for mountain access, remote workers who want an airport and a real downtown, and Nevadans moving up from a starter home. The common thread is wanting outdoor life — skiing, hiking, the river — without giving up city conveniences.

The lay of the land

Reno reads as a collection of distinct neighborhoods more than one city. A few we're asked about most:

Midtown

Reno's most walkable district — restaurants, local shops, and character homes from the 1930s–50s. Fits buyers who'd rather bike to dinner than drive to a mall. Inventory is older and moves fast when priced right.

Old Southwest

Tree-lined streets and some of Reno's most beloved historic homes near the river. Charm comes with older-home maintenance questions — we always talk inspections early here.

South Reno

Newer construction, master-planned conveniences, and easy access to the Summit and Mt. Rose Highway. The default answer for families wanting newer schools and newer builds.

Somersett & Northwest

Golf-course and foothill living on the city's west edge, with dramatic Sierra views. HOA communities dominate — we walk every buyer through what the dues actually cover.

Spanish Springs & Sparks

More home for the money just northeast of Reno, popular with first-time buyers and growing families. Each gets its own full guide on this site.

Straight answers

What buyers ask us about Reno

Is Reno still cheaper than California?

Generally yes for comparable homes, and Nevada has no state income tax — but Reno is no longer a bargain market. The honest comparison depends on which California metro you're leaving and which Reno neighborhood you're targeting. Ask us and we'll run your actual numbers instead of an average.

What should I know about winter?

Reno itself gets modest snow that rarely lingers, while the ski resorts twenty minutes up the hill get feet of it. Homes at higher elevations (Galena, parts of Somersett) see more weather — worth factoring into your search.
