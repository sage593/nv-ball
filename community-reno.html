Living in Reno, Nevada

The Biggest Little City has outgrown its nickname. Between the Sierra Nevada foothills and the Truckee River, Reno mixes a walkable urban core, established mid-century neighborhoods, and fast-growing master-planned communities — with no state income tax and Lake Tahoe forty-five minutes away.

What living here is like

Reno runs on the outdoors. Mornings start on the Truckee River path or the trails above Somersett; winter weekends split between Mt. Rose and Tahoe's resorts. Midtown gives the city a genuine food-and-small-business spine, and the university keeps the town younger than visitors expect. The pace is noticeably gentler than the Bay Area — that's usually the point.

Who Reno fits

Strong fit: remote workers who need an airport and a real downtown; families wanting newer schools and master-planned amenities; California transplants trading commute time for mountain access; anyone whose weekends point uphill.

Honest considerations: wildfire smoke drifts in some summer weeks; winter driving at elevation is real; the arts-and-dining scene, while growing fast, is not San Francisco's; and Reno is no longer the bargain it was a decade ago.

The neighborhoods

Midtown

Reno's most walkable district — restaurants, local shops, and character homes from the 1930s–50s. Inventory is older and moves fast when priced right.

Old Southwest

Tree-lined streets and beloved historic homes near the river. Charm comes with older-home maintenance questions — we talk inspections early here.

South Reno & Damonte Ranch

Newer construction, master-planned conveniences, easy access to the Summit and Mt. Rose Highway. The default answer for families wanting newer everything.

Somersett & Northwest

Golf-course and foothill living on the city's west edge with dramatic Sierra views. HOA communities dominate — we walk every buyer through what the dues cover.

Verdi

Where Reno meets the Sierra — rural feel, city access, and snow that arrives earlier than downtown's.

Straight answers

Reno FAQ

Is Reno a good place to live?

For people who want mountain access, a real downtown, no state income tax, and an international airport in one package — yes, one of the best fits in the West. The honest trade-offs: summer smoke some weeks, winter driving, and prices that have risen with the growth.

Which neighborhood should I look at first?

Depends what you're optimizing for — walkability points to Midtown, new construction to South Reno, views to Somersett, history to Old Southwest. One conversation with us usually narrows it to two or three.
