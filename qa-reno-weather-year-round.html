Relocation · Climate

What is the weather like in Reno year-round?

High-desert, four-season, and sunnier than newcomers expect: warm dry summers with genuinely cool nights, crisp blue falls, winters whose modest valley snow melts in days while the Sierra holds the deep stuff, and springs that can't make up their mind. At about 4,500 feet with low humidity, Reno weather is one of the region's quiet selling points — with one honest caveat worth knowing before you move.

Talk to an agent

The seasons, briefly

Summer: warm days, cool nights, low humidity. Fall: the sleeper favorite. Winter: sun between storms, snow that visits rather than stays. Spring: windy, changeable, green.

Valley vs. mountain

Twenty minutes separates lawn-level flurries from resort snowpack. Buy in the valley and drive to winter — or buy at elevation and live in it. Different homes, different maintenance.

The honest caveat

Some summer weeks bring wildfire smoke from around the West. Locals track air quality apps and plan around it. Know it going in and it won't surprise you.

Straight answers

What is the weather really like in Reno year-round?

Four real seasons, high-desert style: dry warm summers with cool nights, sunny crisp falls, modest valley snow in winter (the ski resorts nearby are another story), and changeable springs. Low humidity, lots of sun, cool evenings at 4,500 feet.

Does it snow in Reno itself?

Yes, modestly — and it usually melts in days. The deep snow stays in the Sierra where you want it. Higher-elevation neighborhoods like Galena see more; ask us before you buy at elevation.

What about wildfire smoke?

Some summer weeks, yes — it varies by year, and locals watch air-quality forecasts the way others watch rain. We tell every relocating client this up front.
