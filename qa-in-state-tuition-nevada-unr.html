Relocation · The college math

Moving to Nevada with college-bound kids: the tuition math

For families timing a move around a student, the structural facts: university residency for tuition is its own legal test — generally requiring around twelve months of genuine Nevada domicile before the term you claim it for, with intent shown through the ordinary moves (licenses, registration, taxes), and with the key wrinkle that a dependent student's residency usually follows the parents' — so the move's timing relative to a high schooler's graduation can decide a year of tuition rates (verify current rules with the university's residency office; they publish them and they decide). The regional bonus worth knowing either direction: the Western Undergraduate Exchange (WUE) offers reduced nonresident rates across member states' participating programs — including for Nevada kids heading outward and California kids heading here. UNR anchors the picture locally; here's the family-planning version.

Time the move right

How residency for tuition works

The general architecture (every university publishes and adjudicates its own): roughly twelve months of bona-fide Nevada domicile before the claimed term, demonstrated intent (driver's license, vehicle registration, voter registration, tax filings — the same checklist as residency generally), and dependent students riding on parents' status. Translation for planners: the family's move date, not the student's enrollment date, usually starts the clock — confirm specifics with the registrar's residency office before building plans on them.

The WUE layer

The Western Undergraduate Exchange gives students from member western states reduced nonresident rates at participating programs — a real discount worth checking in both directions: arriving families bridging a residency year, and Nevada students later shopping the region. Participation and terms vary by school and program; the WUE's own listings are current where articles aren't.

The family sequencing

Where the timeline allows, moving the summer after a student's junior year comfortably beats the senior-spring scramble (twelve months lands before freshman fall); where it doesn't, the bridge options get priced: a WUE rate, one nonresident year then reclassification, or community-college starts with transfer paths. UNR's presence also shapes the housing question — the student-housing purchase guide covers that angle — and the school-year sequencing page handles the younger kids.

Straight answers

How long do you have to live in Nevada to get in-state tuition?

Generally ~12 months of bona-fide domicile before the claimed term, parents' status controlling for dependents — but the university's residency office publishes the rules and decides. Move timing vs. graduation is worth real money.

What is WUE and does it help?

A western-states exchange giving reduced nonresident rates at participating programs — useful as an arrival bridge and for Nevada kids heading out. Check current WUE listings; participation varies.

Should we move before our kid's senior year for tuition?

Comfortable play: the summer after junior year (12 months lands before freshman fall). Otherwise price the bridges — WUE, one nonresident year — against forced timing. Use the university's published rules.
