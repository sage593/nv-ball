Nevada taxes for movers: the complete honest picture

The headline and the whole picture: Nevada levies no personal income tax — wages, retirement income, Social Security, and investment gains pass untaxed at the state level — and funds itself through what it does collect: sales taxes (combined rates around 8% in Washoe), moderate property taxes with annual-increase caps (and the post-purchase adjustment every buyer should budget), vehicle registration's value-based governmental services tax, and the real property transfer tax at sales. For movers the compounding math is the story; for accuracy the details live in our deep guides, every one linked below. What Nevada doesn't tax

Income in every flavor: wages (the headline), retirement withdrawals and pensions and Social Security (the retiree math), and investment gains at the state level — the compounding engine behind the California-exit math and the zero-tax-state comparison Nevada wins on clawbacks.

What it does collect

Sales tax (combined rates around 8% locally — the daily-life tax), property taxes (moderate, increase-capped, with the post-purchase adjustment budgeted from the assessor's math), vehicle registration's value-based tax (newer cars notice), and the transfer tax at sales.

The mover's sequence

Residency established properly (the domicile file), the exit-state's claims understood (California's clawbacks for the applicable), and the CPA conversation timed before the move rather than after — sequencing moves real money, and every page above exists because one mover's question deserved a straight answer.

Straight answers

What taxes do you pay in Nevada?

No income tax — wages, retirement, Social Security, gains all pass at the state level. Collected instead: ~8% sales tax, moderate capped property taxes (post-purchase adjustment budgeted), value-based registration, transfer tax at sales.

Is Nevada really cheaper tax-wise than California?

Structurally yes for most: income-tax tiers vs. zero compounds past any sales-tax offset. Asterisks: purchase-price property math and exit-state claims — CPA models yours.

What tax surprises should new Nevada residents expect?

Three: post-purchase property adjustment, value-priced registration (year one, newer cars), and exit-state reach on pre-move gains. Planning beats mail.
