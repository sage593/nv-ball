For buyers · The crawly questions

Do you need a pest inspection in Northern Nevada?

The honest regional picture: Northern Nevada's dry climate spares us the termite pressure that terrorizes wetter states — subterranean termites exist here but at far lower incidence — while the actual local cast runs carpenter ants in moisture-compromised wood, wood-boring beetles in older framing, rodents (a genuine high-desert constant, especially rural and foothill), and moisture-driven decay wherever irrigation or drainage misbehaves. VA loans typically require the pest inspection regardless; for others it's judgment: older homes, rural properties, and any moisture flags earn it. NV Ball Real Estate Group calls it house-by-house — here's the local field guide.

Get the local read

The actual local cast

Rodents lead the regional threat board (mice and pack rats in rural, foothill, and acreage settings — exclusion is the game), carpenter ants exploit wet wood (deck junctions, leaky-hose-bib walls), wood-boring beetles visit older framing, and true termite findings are the exception that proves the dry-climate rule. Your inspector's moisture meter matters more than the Midwest's horror stories.

When the inspection earns its fee

VA purchases (typically required), century homes (District and Old Southwest bones deserve it), rural and acreage properties (rodent and moisture realities), and any general-inspection moisture flag. Newer suburban homes with clean general inspections rarely need the separate trip.

Reading findings proportionally

Rodent evidence means exclusion work (sealing, not panic); carpenter-ant activity means finding the moisture source first (the ants are the symptom); old beetle exit holes in vintage framing are often historical artifacts. Like every inspection: the translation matters more than the list.

Straight answers

Are termites a problem in Reno?

Far less than wetter states — dry climate suppresses them. The real local cast: rodents (the constant), carpenter ants in wet wood, beetles in old framing. The inspection earns its fee on VA loans, old homes, rural properties, moisture flags.

Is a pest inspection required to buy a house in Nevada?

Not by law generally — VA typically requires it, lenders sometimes ask, judgment covers the rest: old, rural, or moisture-flagged homes earn it.

The inspection found rodent evidence — should I walk away?

Almost never — rural rodent evidence is weather, not scandal: exclusion work, priced in. Active structural damage is the rarer, realer negotiation. We translate which you have.
