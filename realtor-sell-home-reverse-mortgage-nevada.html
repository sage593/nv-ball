For owners & heirs · The payoff sale

Selling a house with a reverse mortgage in Nevada

A reverse mortgage sells like any lien-bearing home — the loan pays off at closing and the owner (or estate) keeps remaining equity — with two wrinkles that reward preparation: the payoff balance grows monthly, and when the borrower has passed, servicers run heir timelines with real deadlines and extension paperwork. NV Ball Real Estate Group handles these sales for owners and for heirs: payoff quote ordered first, servicer communication documented, and the sale paced to the clock that's actually running.

Start with the payoff quote

For owners selling

The mechanics are ordinary — list, sell, loan pays off at closing, you keep the remaining equity. The wrinkle is the growing balance: a current payoff quote anchors the net math before pricing, so the decision is made on real numbers.

For heirs and estates

Servicers run post-passing timelines with deadlines, extensions, and paperwork — manageable, but not ignorable. We've run these: establish contact early, document everything, and pace the sale to the servicer's actual clock. Estate questions pair with our inherited-property practice.

The 95% rule, honestly

When the loan balance exceeds the home's value, federally insured reverse mortgages generally allow a payoff at a percentage of appraised value — meaning underwater heirs usually aren't trapped. Specifics belong to the servicer and an attorney; we run the sale side to their ruling.

Straight answers

Can I sell my house if it has a reverse mortgage?

Yes — it sells like any mortgaged home: loan pays off at closing, you keep the remaining equity. First move: the current payoff quote — the balance grows monthly, and the net math needs the real number.

What happens when a parent with a reverse mortgage passes away?

The loan comes due and the servicer starts a real-but-manageable clock: sell, refinance to keep, or walk. Early contact and documented communication win here — we pace the sale to that clock, alongside the estate's attorney.

What if the house is worth less than the reverse mortgage balance?

Usually heirs aren't trapped — federally insured reverse mortgages generally settle at a percentage of appraised value, not the full balance. Terms come from the servicer; we run the sale mechanics once confirmed.
