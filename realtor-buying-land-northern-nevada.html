Buying land in Northern Nevada: an agent who reads dirt

Land is the purchase where diligence IS the value: the questions that decide a lot's worth — can you legally build (zoning, minimum lot sizes, recorded easements and restrictions read before love), what will services cost (distance to power, water and sewer is the budget's biggest lever; beyond the lines means well and septic, and septic means perc testing that can pass, fail, or constrain where the house goes), and what does the county actually allow — are exactly the ones a house-shopping process never teaches. NV Ball Real Estate Group walks land buyers through the full file, from Palomino Valley parcels to Carson Valley acreage, with feasibility contingencies written into offers so the dirt proves itself before your money commits. Here's how we work it.

Walk a parcel with us

Buildability before beauty

Zoning and allowed uses verified with the county (not the listing), minimum lot sizes and setbacks read, recorded easements and CC&Rs pulled (a conservation easement or access question changes everything), and legal access confirmed — landlocked parcels and handshake driveways are the classic traps. The view comes second; the right to build comes first.

The services math

Distance to power, water and sewer is the number-one cost lever — 'utilities nearby' deserves measurement, not adjectives. Beyond the lines: well feasibility (neighbors' wells tell stories; the rural-systems file), septic with perc testing (results can constrain the building envelope or fail outright — we write offers contingent on it), propane where the pipeline ends, and internet verified, not assumed.

Offers built for feasibility

Land offers earn longer diligence windows: feasibility contingencies covering perc, survey, utility-cost quotes and county verification, with financing shaped to the purchase (land loans run different terms than mortgages — your lender conversation comes early). We've handled parcels across the region's counties; the process is the product.

Straight answers

What should I check before buying land in Nevada?

In order: legal buildability (zoning, lot minimums, easements, confirmed access), services cost (distance to lines; beyond them = well feasibility, perc-tested septic, propane, verified internet), then survey and land-shaped financing — all inside feasibility contingencies.

What is a perc test and do I need one?

It measures soil drainage to determine whether and where septic can go — results can constrain the building site or fail outright. Any lot beyond sewer needs one inside your contingency window.

Is buying land cheaper than buying a house?

The sticker yes, the math it-depends: services, site prep and construction ride on top, and financing differs. We run land-and-build against built-on-acreage before you commit.
