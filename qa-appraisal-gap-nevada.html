Buying & selling · Deal mechanics

What happens if the appraisal comes in low?

The lender lends on the lower of price or appraisal, so a low appraisal turns the difference into real cash — someone's. Four standard resolutions: the seller reduces to appraised value, the buyer brings gap cash, the parties meet between, or the buyer exits under an intact appraisal contingency with earnest money in hand. Which one happens is decided less in the moment than weeks earlier, when the contract allocated the leverage. That's the part we write carefully.

Buyer-side play

Keep the contingency intact unless competition truly demands otherwise — and if offering a gap guarantee, cap it in writing. Ceilings are the discipline.

Seller-side play

Price to appraise, prepare the comp package for the appraiser's visit, and know your fallback before the report lands. Hope is not a strategy; preparation is.

The reconsideration card

Legitimately better comps sometimes move a value. We play the card when evidence supports it — and tell you straight when it won't.

Straight answers

What happens if the appraisal comes in low in Nevada?

Four paths: seller reduces, buyer covers the gap in cash, both meet in the middle, or the buyer exits under an intact appraisal contingency. The lender lends on the lower number, so the gap is real cash — and the contract written weeks earlier decides who holds the leverage.

What is an appraisal gap guarantee?

A written promise to cover gap cash up to a stated ceiling — offer-strengthening in hot moments, dangerous without the ceiling. We size them deliberately.

Can appraisals be challenged?

Sometimes — a reconsideration with genuinely better comps can move one. We attempt it when evidence supports it, and say so plainly when it doesn't.
