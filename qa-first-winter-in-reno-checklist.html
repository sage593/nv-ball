Your first winter in Reno: the practical checklist

Reno winter is manageable — locals barely blink — but it punishes the unprepared in very specific ways: real snow tires (not all-seasons-probably-fine) for anyone driving to Tahoe or living on a hill; home prep before the first freeze (hose bibs, furnace service, duct check); the high-desert combination of intense sun, dry air, and black ice mornings; and the Sierra storm rhythm where valley rain means mountain feet. New arrivals who run this checklist in October have boring winters; the January learners fund the tow companies. NV Ball Real Estate Group hands this list to every relocating client — here's the whole thing.

Get winter-ready properly

The vehicle truths

Dedicated snow tires transform winter here — especially for hill neighborhoods, Tahoe runs, and anything up Geiger or Mt. Rose; chains or traction-rated tires are legally required on mountain passes during controls. AWD helps you go; only tires help you stop.

The house truths

Before first freeze: disconnect hoses and cover bibs, service the furnace, check weatherstripping, know your main water shutoff, and stock ice melt. Hill and acreage homes add: plow plan, generator thought, and pipe-freeze awareness in cold snaps. Twenty October minutes beat February emergencies.

The high-desert specifics

Black ice forms on clear cold mornings after sunny melt days — north-facing streets and bridges first. The sun stays intense at elevation (sunglasses year-round), the air stays dry (humidifier, lotion, water), and valley rain often means feet of snow at the ski gates forty minutes away — which is also the good news.

Straight answers

Do I really need snow tires in Reno?

Valley-floor-only drivers often get by; hill dwellers, early commuters, and anyone Tahoe-bound want real snow tires — the best winter money spent. Passes enforce chain/traction controls in storms. AWD goes; tires stop.

How should I prep my house for a Reno winter?

October: hoses off, bibs covered, furnace serviced, weatherstripping checked, shutoff located, ice melt stocked. Hills/acreage: plow plan + pipe awareness. Twenty minutes vs. February emergencies.

Is driving in Reno winter scary?

Mostly routine — plowed roads, passing storms, modest valley accumulation. Respect: black-ice mornings, bridges, north-facing streets, and storm-day passes (chain controls, occasional closures). Forecast + margin = boring, as intended.
