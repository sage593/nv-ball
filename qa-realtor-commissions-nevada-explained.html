How realtor commissions actually work now: the plain-English version

The honest current picture, post-2024 industry settlement: commissions have always been negotiable and now the structure says so out loud — sellers negotiate what they'll pay their own listing agent, buyer's-agent compensation is no longer advertised through the MLS, buyers sign written representation agreements stating their agent's fee before touring, and whether a seller contributes toward the buyer's side is now an explicit negotiation point in offers (sellers often still do, because it widens the buyer pool — but it's a decision, not a default). What didn't change: the work the fee buys — pricing, marketing, negotiation, escrow management — and the fact that cheap representation that mishandles one negotiation costs more than it saved. We explain our fees in plain English before any agreement; here's the landscape.

Get the plain-English version

What changed in 2024

The national settlement restructured the mechanics: buyer-agent compensation left the MLS listings, written buyer representation agreements (fee stated up front) became the standard before touring, and seller contributions to the buyer's side became an explicit offer-by-offer negotiation. The practical effect: everyone's fee conversation now happens early and in writing — which good agents always preferred anyway.

Who pays what, now

Sellers: negotiate their listing agent's fee in the listing agreement, and decide — offer by offer — whether to contribute toward buyer-side compensation (many still do: it widens the buyer pool and gets negotiated like any other term). Buyers: sign representation agreements stating their agent's fee and how it's satisfied (seller contribution, the buyer directly, or a mix, per the deal). Every number in both paragraphs is negotiable; anyone who says otherwise is negotiating against you.

What the fee actually buys

The honest audit for either side: comp-grounded pricing versus hope, marketing with real reach (our Ylopo stack), minutes-fast inquiry response (Follow Up Boss), negotiation that survives the moments deals die, and escrow management to the keys. The interview questions test whether a given agent's fee buys that — ask us the same ones.

Straight answers

How do real estate commissions work in Nevada now?

Since 2024: sellers negotiate their listing fee; buyer-agent pay left the MLS; buyers sign written fee agreements before touring; seller contributions to the buyer's side are offer-by-offer negotiation. All of it negotiable, early, in writing.

Are realtor commissions negotiable?

Always — now explicitly. The better question is what the fee buys: pricing discipline, marketing reach, response systems, negotiation. That varies more than the percentage.

Do buyers have to pay their agent directly now?

The agreement states the fee; the offer negotiates who funds it — seller contributions remain common. The change is transparency, settled deal by deal.
