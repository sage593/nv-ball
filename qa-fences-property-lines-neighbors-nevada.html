For owners · The shared line

Fences and neighbors in Nevada: who pays, and how to keep the peace

The questions arrive together: who pays for the fence between us (Nevada's practical norm runs shared-benefit-shared-cost by agreement — the neighborly split is custom more than mandate, and the unilateral builder generally funds their own choice), where exactly does the line run (the survey, never the old fence's memory), and what rules govern height and type (local codes and HOA CC&Rs where they apply — front-yard and corner-lot rules surprise people). The meta-skill is the conversation: fence projects shared before built, surveys shown rather than asserted, and agreements in writing keep thousand-dollar projects from becoming five-figure disputes. Here's the practical law-and-custom brief.

Keep my line peaceful

The who-pays reality

Nevada's practical custom: shared fences benefit both, so neighbors commonly split by agreement — and agreement is the operative word: the unilateral builder funds their own project, the neighbor who wants upgrades funds the upgrade's delta, and the conversation-before-construction is where splits get fair. Writing it down (a simple shared email suffices) keeps memories honest.

The line and the rules

Build on the survey, not the old fence (fences wander; surveys don't) — an encroaching new fence is an expensive apology. Height and type rules live in local code (front yards and corner-lot sightlines especially) and HOA CC&Rs where they apply; acreage adds the open-range and livestock-fencing wrinkles rural counties carry.

The dispute off-ramp

When neighbors disagree: survey first (facts calm conversations), the written-offer second (splitting fairly in ink invites reciprocity), mediation before attorneys for the stubborn cases, and perspective throughout — fences cost thousands; feuds cost more and move in next door forever.

Straight answers

Who pays for a fence between neighbors in Nevada?

Agreement over mandate: shared benefit commonly splits by agreement; unilateral builders self-fund; upgraders fund the delta. Conversation first, writing second, survey underneath.

Can my neighbor make me pay for half their new fence?

Generally not unilaterally — no agreement, their project. But fair shared-replacement offers deserve fair consideration; splitters keep fences AND peace.

What rules govern fence height and placement?

Local code + CC&Rs, checked before building. Placement follows the SURVEY — old-fence memory builds legal exhibits.
