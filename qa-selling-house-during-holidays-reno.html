For sellers · The December question

Should I take my house off the market for the holidays?

The December-decision framework: a well-priced listing with traffic should usually stay live — December's buyers are the motivated ones (job starts, lease deadlines, year-end moves), competition thins as other sellers pause, and the middle path handles the family realities: blackout days (Thanksgiving weekend, the Eve-and-Day cluster) pause showings without surrendering the market. The genuine pause cases: listings that were struggling anyway (fix the price or presentation during the break — the relaunch playbook applies), and households that truly can't host showings for weeks. The season's craft: photos before decorations, decor kept light in listings, and escrow calendars padded for the industry's short weeks. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs December listings every year — here's the honest version.

Make my December call

The stay-live case

December's buyer pool is small and serious — deadlines drive them (January starts, expiring leases, tax-year moves) — while the competition thins as other sellers pause: fewer rivals for the buyers who can't wait. A priced-right home with any traffic usually serves the seller better live than dark.

The middle path

Blackout days beat delisting: showings paused for the family cluster (Thanksgiving weekend, the holidays themselves) while the listing works every other day. Buyers and agents understand; the market presence survives; the turkey goes unphotographed. It's the standard play for a reason.

The genuine pause (and the craft)

Struggling listings can use the break — for fixes, not just rest: price and presentation adjusted, then the relaunch timed to January's early-bird buyers. And the seasonal craft either way: photos shot before decorations, listing decor kept light, escrow calendars padded for lender-and-title short weeks (the timeline breathes in December).

Straight answers

Should I take my house off the market during the holidays?

Usually not, if priced and trafficked: December's buyers are the motivated ones, rivals thin, and blackout days cover the family cluster. Pause genuinely to FIX struggling listings for January, not just to rest them.

Do houses actually sell in December in Reno?

Yes — to the deadline-driven. Lighter volume, heavier seriousness; a ratio many sellers quietly prefer.

How should I handle decorations and showings in a listed home?

Photos before decorating, decor lighter than family-normal, blackouts for the true family days, show-ready between. Managed, brief, and worth it.
