For sellers · The hard conversation

Your house isn't selling: when to cut the price, and how

The market communicates in two channels — showings and offers — and the diagnostic is blunt: no showings means the price is wrong at the search-filter level (buyers aren't even clicking; the fix is price, not patience), showings without offers means the price is close but the product-price pairing loses to competition (walkthrough feedback names the gap), and either signal sustained across the first few weeks is data, not noise, because a listing's attention peaks at launch and decays — which is why waiting rarely heals a mispriced listing and why the first cut should be the last one: a reduction big enough to cross a search-filter boundary and re-enter the conversation beats the death-by-a-thousand-trims pattern buyers read as desperation. The honest alternative where price isn't the problem: presentation or access failures have their own fixes. Here's the decision framework.

Diagnose my listing honestly

Reading the two channels

No showings: invisible at the filter level — buyers searching your bracket aren't clicking, and no amount of waiting fixes a filter problem. Showings, no offers: the walkthrough is losing to competition at this price — feedback (we collect it systematically) names whether it's condition, layout or just number. Offers below ask: the market made its statement; negotiate it or re-anchor. Each signal has its own fix; misreading them wastes the listing's best weeks.

How to cut, if cutting

Once, decisively: cross a search-filter boundary so new buyer pools see the listing fresh (a cut that stays inside the same bracket buys nothing), timed early while attention still exists (the launch-decay curve is unforgiving — week three's cut outperforms week nine's), and paired with a relaunch moment (refreshed media if needed, re-pitched to the agent community). Serial small trims read as desperation and train buyers to wait you out.

When price isn't the problem

The honest audit first: access (showing restrictions kill listings quietly — lockbox beats appointment-only), presentation (dark photos and clutter lose clicks the price never sees; the staging math), and the back-on-market stigma cases (managed differently). And the timing honesty: thin-season listings sometimes need patience, not cuts — the seasonal read — which is exactly the judgment call the live data decides.

Straight answers

When should I reduce the price of my house?

No showings = filter-invisible: cut. Showings, no offers = losing at this price: feedback names the gap. Cut early, once, across a search-filter boundary — serial trims train buyers to wait you out.

How much should a price reduction be?

Enough to cross a filter boundary and reach fresh eyes — same-bracket trims buy nothing. One decisive move, sized by live comp work.

Why is my house getting showings but no offers?

You're winning clicks and losing walkthroughs: competition beats your product-price pairing. Feedback names the gap — fix what's fixable, then have the price conversation.
