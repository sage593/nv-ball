For buyers · The new first step

The buyer representation agreement: what it is and how to sign one wisely

If you've toured homes recently, you've met the change: since the industry's 2024 settlement, agents generally need a written buyer agreement before showing homes — a document stating what your agent does, how they're compensated (and by whom — seller offers commonly cover it, but the obligation architecture is now explicit), and for how long you're committed. It's genuinely good consumer policy wearing awkward first-conversation clothes. The wisdom is in the terms: start narrow (short terms, even single-property agreements, are legitimate asks while you evaluate an agent), understand the compensation line, and know your exit. NV Ball Real Estate Group explains ours before asking for ink — here's the field guide.

Sign it wisely

Why it exists now

The 2024 settlement reshaped buyer-side compensation: written agreements before touring are now the professional norm, making explicit what was fuzzy — your agent's duties, pay, and term. Awkward at hello, healthier for everyone measurable; the fuzzy era protected nobody.

The terms that matter

Compensation (the number, and the architecture: seller offers commonly cover it, with the agreement governing shortfalls — read that line twice), term and scope (short initial terms and single-showing agreements are legitimate while evaluating an agent — we offer them), and exit rights in writing.

Signing without surrendering

Reasonable asks any good agent grants: a trial scope before a long commitment, plain answers on the compensation line, and cancellation terms that respect dissatisfaction. An agent who resists those conversations before signing answers your real question early — and cheaply.

Straight answers

Do I have to sign an agreement before an agent shows me homes?

Generally yes, post-settlement: duties, compensation (commonly covered by seller offers — read the shortfall line), and term, in writing before touring. Wise version: short trial scope first, exit rights in ink.

Who actually pays my buyer's agent?

Commonly seller offers still cover it, deal by deal — your agreement governs shortfalls (credits, negotiation, or you: read that line twice). We walk it before ink.

Can I try an agent before committing long-term?

Legitimately — short terms and single-showing agreements are fair asks (we offer them). Demanding six months before showing one house answers your question early.
