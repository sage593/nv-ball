What living here is like

Virginia City is the town the Comstock Lode built — a National Historic Landmark where the boardwalks, saloons, and mine headframes are the real 1860s articles and tourism keeps the lights on. Above it, the Virginia City Highlands run a different economy entirely: one-acre-and-up parcels (ranging to 40-acre spreads in the Highland Ranches) where Reno professionals trade a thirty-minute Geiger Grade commute for horses, workshops, star-dark skies, and wild mustangs wandering through. Together they're Storey County's answer to a question more buyers keep asking: how close can real rural independence sit to a Reno paycheck?

Who Virginia City & the Highlands fits

Strong fit: acreage buyers who want land and privacy within a genuine Reno commute; horse and workshop households; history lovers who'd actually live on C Street's calendar of events; and independent-minded buyers comfortable with wells, septic, generators, and gravel.

Honest considerations: Geiger Grade is a mountain road — beautiful in June, demanding in January, and your daily commute; Highlands living means wells, septic, propane, and wildfire defensible-space work as standing obligations; town services are tourist-scale, with real shopping in Reno or Carson City; and winter at 6,000+ feet arrives earlier and stays later than the valley's.

Virginia City & the Highlands FAQ

Is Virginia City a good place to live?

For the right household — genuinely. You live inside a National Historic Landmark with real community, 30 minutes from Reno. The terms: C Street's tourist seasons, tourist-scale services, and a mountain-road commute. Residents choose the character on purpose.

What should I know before buying in the Virginia City Highlands?

Well-and-septic country: well production, septic condition, propane, road access, defensible space — all standard diligence here, and we run the full checklist. One acre to forty; the lifestyle scales with the parcel.

How is the commute to Reno, honestly?

≈30 minutes down Geiger Grade to south Reno — scenic nine months, serious in winter. Proper tires, forecast awareness, and for most up here, a trade they'd make again every time.
