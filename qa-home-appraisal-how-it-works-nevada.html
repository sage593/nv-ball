For buyers & sellers · The lender's referee

How does a home appraisal work in Nevada?

An appraisal is the lender's independent check that the home is worth the loan: a licensed appraiser inspects the property, selects recent comparable sales, adjusts for differences, and delivers a value conclusion that the financing must respect. It is not a home inspection (condition gets noted, not investigated), not a negotiation party, and not perfectly precise — it's a defensible professional opinion. Both sides can influence it honestly: sellers through condition, documentation, and agent-supplied comparables; buyers through deal structure that anticipates the range. Prepare for the appraisal

What appraisers weigh

Recent comparable sales first — the heart of the method — adjusted for size, condition, location, and features; plus the subject home's measured reality. What they don't weigh: your feelings, your upgrade receipts at face value, or the bidding war's enthusiasm.

Seller-side preparation

Condition visible (the appraiser sees what buyers saw), documentation ready (improvements with dates and costs), and our comparable-sales package supplied — appraisers can consider relevant comps an agent provides. Preparation moves conclusions more than complaints do.

When the number misses

The toolkit by role: reconsideration-of-value requests with better comps, renegotiation, buyer covers a gap, or the structures in our appraisal-gap guide. Misses are a process step, not a crisis — handled weekly here.

Straight answers

What does a home appraiser actually look at?

Comps first — selected and adjusted for size, condition, location, features — plus a measured walk of your home. Condition noted, not investigated. Output: a defensible opinion the loan must respect.

Can a seller influence the appraisal?

Honestly: show the home well, document improvements, supply a real comps package through your agent. What never works: enthusiasm as evidence.

What happens if the appraisal comes in low?

Process, not crisis: reconsideration with better comps, renegotiation, gap coverage, or restructure. The contract's appraisal language decides the toolkit — which is why we write it anticipating this.
