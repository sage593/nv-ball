For sellers · The wire at the end

When do I get my money after closing in Nevada?

The short answer: at or shortly after recording — typically the same day the county records your deed, or the next business day, by wire to your account. The sequence behind it: you sign a day or more before closing, the buyer's funds land in escrow, the deed records with the county (the legal moment the sale completes), and the title company disburses — paying off your loan and liens first, then wiring your net. Signing day isn't money day; recording day is. NV Ball Real Estate Group walks sellers through the exact calendar at contract — here's the general machine.

The sequence

Sign (often a day-plus early, by appointment or mobile notary), buyer funds land, deed records at the county — completion's legal moment — then disbursement: loan payoffs and liens first, your net wired after. Most sellers see funds the day of recording or the next business day; delays live in payoff-timing and wire cutoffs, not mystery.

What your net actually is

Price minus loan payoffs, liens, commissions, transfer tax, prorations, and escrow fees — all previewed on your settlement statement before signing, never a surprise at the wire. The estimated version arrives with the net sheet on day one.

The wire-safety echo

The disbursement wire deserves the same paranoia as the purchase side: your account details delivered to the title company through verified channels, confirmed by phone on known numbers, changed by nobody late in the game. The fraud protocol applies to sellers too — proceeds are exactly what thieves hunt.

Straight answers

How soon after closing do I get my money as a seller in Nevada?

Recording day or the next business day, by wire. The sequence: sign early, buyer funds land, deed records (the legal moment), payoffs clear, your net wires. Signing day ≠ money day; recording day is.

Why is there a gap between signing and getting paid?

Signing stages documents; recording completes sales — and recording waits on funds, lender instructions, and the county's run. A day or two of machinery, not suspense.

Can anything delay my proceeds after recording?

Mundane culprits: late payoff figures, wire cutoffs, verification steps (protecting you). Build one buffer day into any must-have-funds plan.
