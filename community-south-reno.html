What living here is like

South Reno is where the region's newcomers most often land, and it shows in the best way: newer everything, planned parks and trails, and a retail spine at The Summit that saves the drive downtown. Weekends tilt toward the mountain — Mt. Rose is a direct run up the highway — while the wetlands trails behind Damonte Ranch handle the weekday dog miles.

Who South Reno fits

Strong fit: families wanting newer schools and HOA-kept parks; relocating professionals who want turn-key and low-surprise; skiers who count minutes to Mt. Rose; buyers who'd rather maintain less and live more.

Honest considerations: HOA rules and dues come with most neighborhoods — read them before you fall in love; commutes to downtown or the university run against traffic but exist; character homes are not the local product; and higher-elevation edges catch more weather than the valley floor.

South Reno FAQ

Is South Reno a good place to live?

Yes — it's the region's default for newer-everything: homes, schools, parks, and a straight shot to Mt. Rose. Trade-offs: HOA life and not much historic charm.

What is Damonte Ranch like?

South Reno's flagship master-planned community — parks, wetlands trails, newer schools. Its own full guide is next on our list.

How is the commute downtown?

Usually 15–20 minutes up I-580 — and plenty of South Reno residents work south of town anyway.
