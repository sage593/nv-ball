For new owners · The house's rhythm

New homeowner maintenance in Nevada: the high-desert calendar

The house you just bought runs on a seasonal rhythm this climate sets: fall is the big ask (hoses off and bibs covered before first freeze, furnace serviced, weatherstripping checked, irrigation blown out where systems demand it), spring reverses it (irrigation revival, swamp-cooler rituals where they survive, the roof's post-winter look), summer asks for defensible-space attention in the interface zones and filter discipline through fire-season air, and winter mostly asks that you did fall's list. Add the anytime fundamentals — shutoff locations learned before emergencies, filters on schedule, water heater's annual minute — and ownership here is boringly manageable. NV Ball Real Estate Group hands this calendar to every buyer at closing; here's the whole thing.

Get the full calendar

Fall: the big ask

Before first freeze: hoses disconnected, bibs insulated, irrigation winterized (blown out where systems require — the burst-line repair bill funds the service many times over), furnace serviced and filtered, weatherstripping and sweep checks. One October Saturday; a winter of not thinking about it. The first-winter checklist overlaps on purpose.

Spring and summer's asks

Spring: irrigation revived and leak-checked, the roof's post-winter inspection (binoculars suffice), gutters cleared of winter's deposits. Summer: defensible space maintained in interface zones (the annual obligation, not the one-time project), HVAC filters through smoke-season air, and the dryness-driven caulk-and-seal patrol.

The anytime fundamentals

Learned before needed: main water shutoff, gas shutoff, panel layout. Scheduled quarterly: filters. Annually: water-heater flush-or-check, smoke and CO batteries, the resilience kit's once-over. The whole fundamentals list fits on an index card; we provide the card.

Straight answers

What maintenance does a house need in Nevada's climate?

The calendar: fall's the big ask (freeze prep: hoses, bibs, irrigation blow-out, furnace), spring reverses (revival, roof look, gutters), summer = defensible space + smoke-season filters, plus the index-card fundamentals (shutoffs, filters, water heater).

What do new Nevada homeowners forget most?

October, year one: un-winterized bibs and irrigation lines teach coastal arrivals expensive lessons. Second: defensible space as annual rhythm, not one-time project.

How much should I budget for home maintenance?

Houses laugh at generic percentages — age, systems, and acreage set the real number. New tract: the calendar. Rural: the systems' schedules. Century home: elder care. Age-appropriate versions at closing.
