For buyers & sellers · The moving-day fights

What stays with the house in Nevada? Fixtures, explained

The rule sounds simple: fixtures — things attached to the house — stay; personal property goes. The fights come from the seams: the mounted TV (mount stays, TV usually goes), the custom curtains (rods stay, drapes arguable), the playset, the hot tub, the garage shelving, the doorbell camera half the neighborhood fights about now. The actual answer is always the contract: anything named explicitly follows the ink, whatever the general rule says. NV Ball Real Estate Group writes inclusions and exclusions specifically on every deal — because the walkthrough is a terrible place to discover a disagreement.

Put it in ink

The fixture rule

Attached-with-intent-of-permanence stays: built-ins, mounted light fixtures, landscaping, hardware. Freestanding goes: furniture, most appliances by local custom unless included, the art on the walls. The test is attachment and intent — and the seams are where the stories live.

The famous fights

Mounted TVs (mount = fixture; TV = usually not), curtain rods versus curtains, smart doorbells and cameras, garage systems, hot tubs, playsets, the beloved rose bushes. Every one of these has ruined a walkthrough somewhere — every one is preventable with a sentence of contract language.

The contract beats the rule

Whatever the doctrine says, explicit inclusions and exclusions govern: 'washer, dryer, and refrigerator included; sellers' dining chandelier excluded.' We write the specifics both directions — sellers list heirlooms early, buyers name what they're counting on — and the walkthrough stays boring.

Straight answers

What is considered a fixture when selling a house?

Attached-with-permanence stays: built-ins, mounted lights, fans, landscaping, hardware. Freestanding goes. The seams — TVs, curtains, doorbells, hot tubs — cause the fights; contract ink settles them in advance.

Do appliances stay with the house in Nevada?

Built-ins stay; freestanding washer/dryer/fridge follow the contract and get negotiated both ways. The safe answer is always the written one — we name them explicitly, every offer.

The sellers took something that should have stayed — now what?

At walkthrough: closing item — return, replace, or credit before recording, while leverage exists. After closing: demand letter, enforceable, annoying. Upstream prevention — specific ink + real walkthrough — beats both.
