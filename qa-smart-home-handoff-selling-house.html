For buyers & sellers · The digital walkthrough

Selling a smart home: the tech handoff nobody plans

The house now has accounts: doorbells and cameras, locks and thermostats, garage controllers and irrigation timers — and the sale needs a digital handoff as deliberate as the key exchange. Sellers: hardwired devices generally convey as fixtures (the fixture rules apply), and your obligations include factory-resetting and releasing accounts — walking away still connected to the cameras in someone else's home is a problem with a name. Buyers: assume nothing is reset until you've reset it, change every code, and claim every account. NV Ball Real Estate Group added the digital walkthrough to our closing checklist years ago — here it is.

Run the digital walkthrough

What conveys

Hardwired and installed devices — video doorbells, integrated cameras, smart locks, thermostats, built-in hubs — generally stay as fixtures; plug-in gadgets follow the contract. We name the significant ones explicitly in offers, because 'smart home' in a listing is a promise someone has to keep.

The seller's handoff duties

Factory-reset each device or formally transfer its account, remove them from your apps, hand over hub credentials and any subscriptions' status, and document it. Remaining connected to a sold home's cameras or locks isn't a quirk — it's a privacy violation waiting for a headline. The reset list takes an hour; do it.

The buyer's day-one protocol

Trust nothing inherited: reset devices you can, change every lock code and garage PIN, claim accounts, and replace what won't release cleanly (orphaned devices tied to dead accounts are a known plague). Our closing packet includes the device-by-device checklist — boring, fast, and genuinely important.

Straight answers

Do smart home devices stay with the house when it sells?

Installed/hardwired: generally convey as fixtures. Plug-ins: follow the contract. Name the significant ones explicitly — and treat accounts like keys.

What should a seller do about smart devices before closing?

Reset or transfer every account, remove the home from your apps, hand over credentials, document it. Staying connected to sold-house cameras is a problem with your name on it — spend the hour.

As a buyer, how do I secure an inherited smart home?

Day one: new codes everywhere, reset-and-claim every device, replace the orphans that won't release. Assume nothing was reset until YOU reset it.
