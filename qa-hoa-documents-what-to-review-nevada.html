HOA documents: what to actually review before you buy

Nevada law gives buyers into HOA communities a resale package and a review window — a real right most buyers waste on a skim. The documents that deserve the actual read: the budget and reserve study (is the association saving for the roof, or planning to special-assess you for it?), the CC&Rs (what you can't do with your own property), recent meeting minutes (where the unspoken problems speak), litigation and insurance status (lender-killers hide here), and the fee-and-violation schedule. NV Ball Real Estate Group reads packages beside our buyers — here's the triage.

Read my package with me

The money documents

Budget, reserves, and the reserve study: healthy associations fund tomorrow's roofs today; unhealthy ones defer until a special assessment lands on whoever owns at the wrong time. Reserve adequacy is the single best predictor of your future surprise bills — we read it first.

The rules you're marrying

CC&Rs govern rentals (investors: caps and minimums live here), parking, pets, paint, landscaping, and improvements. Most are livable; some are dealbreakers for your plans specifically — RV owners, landlords, and home-business runners should read their lines before loving the house.

The quiet tells

Recent minutes reveal what budgets hide: looming projects, simmering disputes, management chaos. Litigation and insurance status decide whether lenders will even play — the condo pages cover that mechanic. Five documents, one evening, decades of consequences.

Straight answers

What HOA documents should I review before buying in Nevada?

Five things in your review window: budget + reserve study (special assessments hide in weak reserves), CC&Rs (rentals, parking, pets), recent minutes (problems speak here), litigation/insurance status, fee schedules. One evening; decades of consequences.

What are the HOA red flags worth walking away over?

Underfunded reserves + aging infrastructure, structural litigation, chronic deficits, and CC&R terms that kill YOUR plan (rental caps, the classic). The flag isn't the fee — it's fee-versus-what-it-funds.

Can I get out of a purchase after reading the HOA documents?

That's what the window is FOR — a statutory exit if the documents reveal the wrong marriage. The right exists for readers; skimmers waive it in practice.
