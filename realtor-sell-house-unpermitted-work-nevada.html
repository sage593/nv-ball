Selling a house with unpermitted work in Nevada

It's sellable — but the work shapes the plan. Unpermitted additions and conversions must be disclosed under Nevada's seller-disclosure framework, lenders and appraisers treat them unevenly (some value them at zero, some balk entirely), and your realistic options are: legalize what can be retroactively permitted, price the house on its permitted footprint with the extra as upside, or market to the buyers — cash, renovators — who price it themselves. NV Ball Real Estate Group walks the actual permit history first, then picks the path that nets you most.

Walk my permit history

Disclosure is non-negotiable

Known unpermitted work goes on the disclosure form — full stop. Disclosed-and-priced sells; discovered-in-escrow renegotiates, and discovered-after-closing litigates. We write it plainly and price around it.

The retroactive option

Some work can be legalized after the fact — permits, inspections, corrections — and sometimes that cost is the highest-return prep money you can spend, because it unlocks financed buyers. We'll tell you honestly whether yours is that case.

Pricing the honest footprint

Appraisers often credit the permitted square footage and treat the rest as unvalued bonus. Pricing on the permitted footprint with the extra marketed as upside keeps appraisals — and escrows — intact.

Straight answers

Can I sell a house with unpermitted work in Nevada?

Yes — disclosed and strategized: legalize what can be legalized, or price the permitted footprint with the extra as bonus, or sell to cash/renovation buyers. Pretending is the only wrong move.

Will the buyer's lender care about an unpermitted addition?

Often — appraisers commonly zero-value unpermitted footage, and some lenders balk outright. That quietly shrinks your buyer pool, which is why we walk permits before pricing, not mid-escrow.

Is it worth getting permits after the fact?

Sometimes the best prep money there is — legalized space appraises, financed buyers return. Sometimes corrections outrun the value. Permit-office conversation + honest math first; then spend.
