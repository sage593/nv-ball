For buyers · The price-check instinct

Is this house overpriced? How buyers actually tell

The read is learnable: pull the genuinely comparable recent sales (same area, size band, condition tier — not the seller's aspirational cousins), check the price-per-foot against them with adjustments a human would make, and weigh the tells that travel with overpricing — long days on market in a moving segment, price history showing hopeful cuts, and listing language working harder than the photos justify. Overpriced doesn't mean untouchable: sellers anchored high often negotiate reasonably once an evidence-based offer opens the conversation. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs this read on every house our buyers like — here's the method.

Run the price check

The comp discipline

Recent sales, not active listings (asking isn't getting); genuinely similar, not aspirationally similar; adjusted for the differences that matter (condition, lot, location micro-factors). Twenty minutes of honest comps answers most price questions — we run them before every offer, free.

The tells beyond the number

Days on market outrunning the segment's norm, a price history of hopeful trims, relisting resets, and marketing adjectives doing heavy lifting. None proves overpricing alone; together they tell you the market already voted — and how the seller's been taking the news.

Making the offer anyway

Wanting an overpriced house isn't a mistake; paying the overprice is. The approach: an evidence-based offer with the comps attached (politely — data opens conversations that lowballs close), terms that add appeal, and patience — overpriced listings soften on their own calendar. The negotiation usually finds the real number.

Straight answers

How can I tell if a house is overpriced?

Comps first: recent, genuinely similar SALES (not askings), honestly adjusted. Then the tells: long DOM for the segment, hopeful-trim price history, adjectives outworking photos. The market's usually already voted.

Should I avoid overpriced listings entirely?

No — often opportunities: thinner competition, softening sellers, evidence-based offers that open conversations. Want the house; pay the comps' number. Patience + data wins these.

Why do sellers overprice?

Human reasons: the neighbor's outlier sale, improvement-cost hopes, flattering online estimates, or an agent who bought the listing with a number. Knowing which story you face shapes the talk.
