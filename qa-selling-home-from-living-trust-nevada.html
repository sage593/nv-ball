For trustees · The administration sale

Selling a house from a living trust in Nevada: the trustee's guide

This is the sale the trust was built to enable: when a home sits in a living trust, the successor trustee can typically sell it without probate court — the trust document itself, a death certificate, and trustee-authority paperwork (often a certification of trust, sometimes an affidavit identifying the successor) satisfy escrow and title, and the sale proceeds flow into the trust for distribution. It's dramatically simpler than probate, and still a real process: trustee duties run to the beneficiaries, documentation must be right, and tax treatment (the basis step-up especially) deserves professional eyes. NV Ball Real Estate Group handles trust sales regularly — here's the checklist.

Run the trustee's checklist

Why trust sales skip court

The trust owns the home, and its document names who acts when the grantor dies — so authority transfers by paperwork, not probate petition: certification of trust, death certificate, successor-trustee documentation. Title and escrow verify and proceed. This is the payoff of the planning; probate's timeline and cost stay on the shelf.

The trustee's duties

You act for the beneficiaries: market-supported pricing (an appraisal or documented valuation protects you), communication with beneficiaries documented, proceeds to the trust's terms, and no self-dealing shortcuts. The fiduciary frame isn't ceremony — it's your protection when someone later asks questions. The trust's attorney stays in the loop; we work alongside.

The tax conversation

Inherited homes generally receive a basis step-up to date-of-death value — often making a prompt sale largely tax-light — but the specifics (trust type, timing, appreciation since death) belong to the estate's CPA. We time the sale to the tax advice, not the reverse, and the inherited-property guide covers the probate sibling.

Straight answers

Can a successor trustee sell a house in Nevada without probate?

Typically yes — that's what the trust was FOR: authority by paperwork (certification of trust, death certificate), not probate petition. What remains: fiduciary duties, clean documentation, and the step-up tax conversation.

What documents will escrow need for a trust sale?

Certification of trust (or the document), death certificate, successor-trustee documentation — signed in trustee capacity. Title reviews early; we order it at listing.

Do I owe taxes when selling my parent's house from their trust?

Often less than feared — the date-of-death basis step-up does heavy lifting on prompt sales. Specifics (trust type, timing) belong to the estate's CPA; we calendar to their advice.
