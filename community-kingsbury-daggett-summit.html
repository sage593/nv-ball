What living here is like

Kingsbury Grade climbs from the Carson Valley over Daggett Summit and drops toward the lake — and strung along its upper reaches sit Tahoe's ski-side neighborhoods: forested benches of mountain homes and condo clusters within minutes of Heavenly's Nevada-base lodges, where winter means walking or shuttling to chairlifts and summer means trailheads from the driveway. The Grade's two-world geometry is the quiet superpower: lake and Stateline ten-fifteen minutes one way, Carson Valley's services twenty-five the other — with Nevada's tax treatment covering all of it.

Who Kingsbury & Daggett Summit fits

Strong fit: ski-first households and second-home owners — Heavenly's Nevada lodges are the organizing amenity; mountain-living romantics who mean it (altitude, snow, pines, quiet); STR-curious investors (rules vary — we verify current Douglas County code per address); and Carson Valley commuters who want Tahoe addresses with valley access over the summit.

Honest considerations: altitude is the lifestyle's price — snow loads, plowing plans, winter driving on the Grade (beautiful, steep, occasionally chain-controlled), and shoulder-season mud; condo purchases carry association diligence we run hard (mountain buildings have mountain budgets); STR rules are jurisdiction-specific and shifting — verified per parcel, never assumed; and services at altitude are minimal — the lake or the valley supplies them.

Kingsbury & Daggett Summit FAQ

Is Kingsbury a good place to live or buy at Tahoe?

For ski-first buyers — the purpose-built answer: Heavenly's lodges in minutes, lake down one side of the Grade, valley services down the other, Nevada taxes throughout. Terms: real altitude living — snow, winter driving, mud season, minimal on-mountain services.

Can I short-term rent a Kingsbury property?

Douglas County's Tahoe rules have shifted — caps, permits, zones. We verify current code per parcel before any offer; a printed generality would mislead someone.

How is the Grade in winter, honestly?

Well-maintained and still demanding respect: steep, switchbacked, sometimes chain-controlled, rarely closed. Proper tires + forecast awareness = routine. For the right households, the Grade is the sorting mechanism.
