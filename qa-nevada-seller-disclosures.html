Selling · The paperwork that matters

What do sellers have to disclose in Nevada?

The centerpiece is the Seller's Real Property Disclosure Form — the SRPD — where you disclose known defects and conditions across the home's systems, answered from your actual knowledge and delivered to buyers on the statutory timeline. Around it orbit the situational disclosures: HOA resale packages in common-interest communities, open range in rural areas, wells and septic, lead paint for pre-1978 homes. The counterintuitive truth: thorough disclosure is seller protection. It's the shortcuts that follow people after closing.

Straight answers

What must sellers disclose in Nevada?

The centerpiece is the Seller's Real Property Disclosure (SRPD): known defects and conditions, answered from your actual knowledge, delivered on the statutory timeline. Around it: HOA resale packages, open range, well/septic, pre-1978 lead paint, and more by situation. Honest completeness protects you; shortcuts follow you.

What if I genuinely don't know about an item?

'Don't know' is an honest, allowed answer — the form asks what you actually know. Knowing and not saying is the unforgivable version.

Can disclosure problems unwind a sale?

Yes — before and after closing. Which is why every disclosure set we send out gets a line-by-line review first. Boring, and bulletproof.
