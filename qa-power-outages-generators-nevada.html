Power outages and generators: the Nevada resilience brief

The acronym worth knowing before you buy in the hills: PSOM — NV Energy's Public Safety Outage Management — the program that proactively shuts off power in designated zones during extreme fire weather (high winds, low humidity, dry fuels), with announced windows that can run hours to a day-plus; emergency de-energizations during active fires add the no-notice version. Translation for buyers: city-grid addresses mostly shrug, while mountain, foothill-interface, and rural addresses should know their zone and plan accordingly — which is a decision tree, not a panic: from battery-backup basics through portable generators to automatic standby systems. NV Ball Real Estate Group flags the zone question in diligence — here's the honest brief.

Plan my address's resilience

The shutoff reality

PSOM events are weather-triggered and typically announced: designated zones (mountain and interface areas prominently) lose power preventively during extreme fire conditions, for stretches that have run hours to beyond a day. Emergency de-energizations during active fires arrive faster. Knowing whether a candidate address sits in a managed zone is a diligence question we ask — the utility publishes the program; we read it against your address.

The decision tree

Tier one: battery basics (communications, medical devices, the router remote work dies without). Tier two: portable generator plus transfer awareness (fuel, safe operation — carbon monoxide discipline is non-negotiable). Tier three: automatic standby systems (propane-plumbed, whole-home or circuits) — real money that mountain full-timers and medical-need households justify readily. Your address's zone, your household's needs, your budget: the tree sorts fast.

The purchase-time questions

Does the address sit in a managed shutoff zone or outage-history area (neighbors know; we ask), does existing backup equipment convey (fixture rules apply — standby systems typically stay, portables typically don't), and does the energy stack (propane especially) support the backup you'd want? Purchase-time answers beat January discoveries.

Straight answers

Does NV Energy shut off power during fire season?

Yes — PSOM: weather-triggered preventive shutoffs in designated zones (typically announced, hours to a day-plus), plus no-notice emergency de-energizations near active fires. Mountain and interface addresses carry it; know your zone at purchase.

Do I need a generator in Reno?

City grid: usually no. Mountain/interface/rural: run the tree — batteries → portable → automatic standby — by zone, household needs, and budget. We flag it at purchase.

Does a backup generator add home value?

In the zones that need them — yes: standby systems convey, read as turnkey resilience, and pre-answer diligence. Portables don't count; plumbed systems do.
