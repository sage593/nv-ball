For buyers & sellers · Deal structure

Seller concessions and rate buydowns in Nevada, explained

Yes — sellers in Nevada can contribute to a buyer's costs, and in today's market many do: closing-cost credits, repair credits, and rate buydowns that cut the buyer's monthly payment without touching the sticker price. Loan programs cap how much a seller may contribute, and the smart question is rarely "discount or credit?" in the abstract — it's which structure nets each side more. NV Ball Real Estate Group negotiates these structures weekly, on both sides.

Structure my deal

What a buydown actually does

A temporary buydown (like a 2-1) lowers the buyer's rate for the first years; a permanent one buys points for the loan's life. Seller-funded, it can beat an equivalent price cut for the buyer's monthly math — your lender prices both, we negotiate the winner.

What sellers should know

A credit that keeps your sticker price intact can cost less than the price cut that gets the same buyer — and comp better for the neighborhood. We run the net sheet both ways before you respond to any offer.

The caps and the fine print

Loan programs limit seller contributions, and appraisals still have to support the price. We structure within the rules so credits survive underwriting instead of unraveling in week three.

Straight answers

Can the seller pay my closing costs in Nevada?

Yes — common and negotiable, within your loan program's caps. Whether a credit beats an equivalent price cut is a math question your lender and we answer before the offer goes out.

What is a 2-1 buydown?

Year one: two points below the note rate. Year two: one below. Year three: the real rate — which is the number to judge your comfort by. Often seller-funded; refinance if rates fall first.

Should I ask for a lower price or a credit?

Run both. Credits aimed at your rate often move the monthly more than the same dollars off price — but not always. Per-deal math, not philosophy; we calculate before we negotiate.
