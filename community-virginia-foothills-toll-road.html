What living here is like

Southeast of the Damonte roundabouts, where Toll Road and Rhodes Road climb toward the Virginia Range, Reno keeps one of its best-kept practical secrets: the Virginia Foothills' streets of larger lots and horse properties, semi-rural in rhythm but ten minutes from the Summit's commerce and fifteen from Midtown. It's Golden Valley's southern twin — the space-without-full-rural-commitment answer for the city's south side — with the Toll Road corridor adding bench-land customs and the range's trails rising behind.

Who Virginia Foothills & Toll Road fits

Strong fit: south-side space-seekers — the southern mirror of Golden Valley's brief: elbow room, animals possible, city reachable; horse households wanting south Reno commutes; buyers hunting the value band between Damonte's HOA polish and true acreage's systems commitment; and trail people (the Virginia Range starts behind the back fence).

Honest considerations: services are mixed — municipal pockets and well/septic streets interleave, so we verify every parcel rather than assume; the benches catch wind and winter a notch harder than the valley floor; lot rules for animals vary street by street (parcel-level confirmation before any horse plan); and polish-seekers belong in Damonte — this pocket sells space and quiet, not curb-appeal uniformity.

Virginia Foothills & Toll Road FAQ

Is the Virginia Foothills a good place to live?

South Reno's best-kept space answer: larger lots, horses possible, Summit commerce in ten. Terms: mixed services (parcel-verified), bench wind and winter, space-over-polish aesthetics.

Can I keep horses there?

Many streets host them — but zoning varies street by street, so plans get parcel-level confirmation before offers. Standard semi-rural practice.

Virginia Foothills or Damonte Ranch?

Space vs. polish: Foothills = elbow room, animals, quiet. Damonte = planned amenities, newer stock, HOA order. Budgets overlap; your Saturday decides.
