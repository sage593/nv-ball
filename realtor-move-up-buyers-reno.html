Outgrowing your house: the move-up plan, both sides managed

The move-up move — starter home sold, bigger home bought, usually with kids and a timeline attached — is really one decision wearing two transactions: sequencing. Sell first and you're a clean, cash-strong buyer who needs somewhere to land (rent-backs and flexible closings shrink the gap); buy first and you carry two payments while the old house sells (bridge-style options and contingent offers exist, with trade-offs we price honestly); and the sequencing guide runs the full decision tree. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs both sides as one plan — the listing and the search on the same calendar, the equity math done before the touring starts, and one team accountable for the whole chain. Here's the move-up method.

Plan both sides at once

The sequencing decision

Sell-first buys negotiating strength and a known budget (your equity, realized) at the cost of interim housing questions — rent-backs negotiated into your sale are the standard bridge. Buy-first buys continuity at the cost of double-carry risk and weaker offers (contingent offers compete at a discount). Your reserves, risk tolerance and the current market's temperature pick; we run the tree with real numbers in conversation.

The equity math first

Before touring: what the starter nets (comp-grounded, costs included — the full cost sheet), what that equity does as a down payment, and what the move-up payment actually looks like at current rates (your lender's live math, locked when it counts — the rate-lock framework). Touring before this math is how families fall in love with the wrong budget.

One team, one chain

The listing prepped while the search runs, closings coordinated to the same week where the plan wants it, the school-calendar sequencing honored for the kids, and a single accountable team when timing wobbles — chains break at the handoffs, so we remove the handoffs. 93 homes sold in the last twelve months includes plenty of these; the choreography is the product.

Straight answers

How does buying a bigger house while selling mine work?

One decision, two transactions: sell-first (strong buyer + rent-back bridge) vs buy-first (continuity + double-carry risk). Equity math before touring; both sides on one calendar.

Should I sell my house before buying a new one?

Usually — sell-first converts equity to strength, rent-backs cover the gap. Buy-first fits strong reserves and a house that won't wait. Priced both ways, live, before committing.

Can I use my home's equity for the next down payment?

That's the engine: net proceeds become the down payment — so compute realistic net first. Pre-sale equity access exists (bridge products, HELOCs) at prices your lender quotes early.
