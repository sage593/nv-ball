Relocation · The Oregon exit

Moving from Portland to Reno: the honest comparison

Portland movers arrive in Reno with the strongest tax contrast in the West — Oregon's steep state income tax against Nevada's none — plus housing that stretches further and sun after the long gray. The honest other half: Reno's food-and-arts scene is smaller (and improving), the landscape is high desert, not forest-green, and the movers who thrive here wanted that change. NV Ball Real Estate Group will tell you plainly which kind of mover you are.

Talk through the move

The tax contrast

Oregon taxes income at some of the nation's higher state rates; Nevada taxes none. For working households and equity events alike, that line moves real money — your CPA quantifies it, we execute the move.

The lifestyle trade

The long gray for ~250 sunny days, the Cascades at a distance for the Sierra up close, curated Portland depth for sleeves-up Reno energy. Midtown is where the two cities' tastes overlap.

Do the visit right

Come twice — once in summer for Tahoe's argument, once in winter for honesty's. The relocation guide builds the itinerary and the plan.

Straight answers

Why are people moving from Portland to Reno?

The ledger: Oregon's steep income tax versus Nevada's none, housing that stretches, sun after the long gray, the Sierra at closer range. Trade-offs, honestly: a smaller food-and-arts scene, high-desert dryness, and growth-era traffic of our own.

How do the cultures compare?

Portland curates; Reno does. Sleeves-up, outside-by-8am, with a food scene that's genuinely improving (Midtown carries it). If Portland's depth of restaurants-venues-weird is your oxygen, calibrate and visit first.

Does the tax math actually favor the move?

For Oregon earners, often substantially — one of the country's higher state income taxes versus none. Sequencing (residency, where work happens, when equity realizes) is CPA territory; we run the real estate to their plan.
