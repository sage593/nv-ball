For married buyers · The vesting line

How should a married couple hold title in Nevada?

Nevada is a community property state, and the vesting line on your deed carries real consequences: most married couples here choose community property with right of survivorship — which combines automatic transfer to the surviving spouse (skipping probate, like joint tenancy) with community property's tax treatment, widely understood to provide a basis step-up on both halves at the first death, a meaningful difference from joint tenancy's one-half step-up. The deed must say it explicitly; defaults don't grant it. The choice is genuinely consequential and genuinely CPA-and-attorney territory — NV Ball Real Estate Group makes sure the conversation happens before escrow closes, not after someone's estate discovers the difference.

Get the vesting right

The menu for married couples

Community property with right of survivorship (the common recommendation — survivorship plus community-property tax treatment), plain community property (willable halves, no automatic transfer), joint tenancy (survivorship with different tax character), and trust vesting (the estate-planning route many land on). Each has real cases; none is a checkbox.

Why CPWROS gets recommended

It pairs probate-skipping survivorship with the tax treatment advisors widely prefer for appreciated homes — the both-halves basis adjustment at the first death that joint tenancy doesn't match. The mechanics belong to your CPA; our job is ensuring the deed language actually says what you chose.

The trust alternative

Couples with living trusts typically vest the home in the trust — probate avoidance, incapacity planning, and privacy in one move, with transfer-tax exemptions covering the move into your own trust. Estate attorney's architecture; we execute the paperwork at either end.

Straight answers

What is the best way for a married couple to hold title in Nevada?

Most commonly CPWROS: survivorship without probate + community property's both-halves basis treatment at first death. The deed must SAY it; defaults don't grant it. CPA and attorney weigh in; trust-holders vest in the trust.

What's the difference between joint tenancy and community property with right of survivorship?

Same survivorship, different tax character: CPWROS adjusts basis on the whole property at first death; joint tenancy on half. On appreciated homes, real money — get professional eyes on the line.

Can we change how we hold title later?

Yes — routine deeds, often exempt between spouses and into your own trust. The catch: nobody remembers until it matters. Review vesting with your estate plan.
