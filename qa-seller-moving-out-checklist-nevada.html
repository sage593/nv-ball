For sellers · The final mile

The seller's move-out checklist: leaving right

The contract's phrase is 'broom-clean' and its meaning is practical: belongings gone (including the garage's archaeology and the backyard's projects), surfaces swept and wiped, and the home delivered as the walkthrough expects — not deep-cleaned to forensic standards, but honestly empty and tidy. The craft beyond the minimum: the handoff kit (every key and remote, garage codes, the smart-home transfers, the manuals-and-paint-colors drawer, utility notes), the timing (moved out before the final walkthrough, not racing the recording), and the spirit — buyers remember how a house was left, and so do the agents who bring you your next deal. Here's the whole list.

Leave it right

Broom-clean, defined

Everything yours: gone (sheds, attics, crawl spaces, the side yard — 'conveys' was for fixtures, not forgotten kayaks); surfaces swept, counters wiped, obvious debris out. Not required: professional deep cleaning — though sellers who leave it show-clean get remembered kindly. Required: the house the walkthrough photographed.

The handoff kit

One drawer or envelope: all keys (doors, mailbox, sheds), remotes and openers, alarm and garage codes, smart-device transfers done (the digital walkthrough), appliance manuals, paint colors and spare materials' location, utility and service notes (trash day, sprinkler quirks, the HVAC filter size). Ten minutes assembling; years of buyer gratitude.

The timing that works

Out before the final walkthrough (racing the recording is how walkthroughs find half-packed garages and deals find drama), utilities per the closing sheet (off your name at recording, never before — the timeline explains the gap), and rent-backs honored to their written day. The last impression is part of the transaction.

Straight answers

What does broom-clean condition mean when selling a house?

All yours removed (garage/attic/yard included), swept, wiped, debris gone — honestly empty, not forensically cleaned. The walkthrough's expectations are the standard.

What should I leave for the buyers?

The envelope: keys, remotes, codes, smart transfers done, manuals, paint colors, the unwritten service notes. Ten minutes; transforms their week one.

When exactly do I have to be out?

Per contract — practically, before the final walkthrough. Racing the recording grows drama. Rent-backs change it in writing; nothing else does.
