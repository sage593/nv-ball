Buying & selling · The fine print

Are home warranties worth it in Nevada?

Sometimes — understood correctly: a home warranty is budget predictability for listed systems and appliances, wrapped in service fees, exclusions, and claim processes, not insurance against everything breaking. The strong cases: older homes with aging systems, and first-year owners who value a known number over surprise bills. The weak ones: newer homes still under builder warranty, and owners happy to self-insure. Seller-paid warranties are usually worth accepting — and every version is worth reading before the first claim, not during it.

Straight answers

Are home warranties worth it in Nevada?

Sometimes — as budget predictability, not insurance. Best case: older systems, first-year buyers. Weak case: newer homes already under builder warranty. Seller-paid versions are worth accepting — and the exclusions page is worth reading before you ever rely on one.

What do warranty complaints usually involve?

The hope-versus-contract gap: exclusions, caps, repair-not-replace calls, dispatch waits. The coverage document read before closing prevents most of it.

Should sellers offer one?

On older homes, often a cheap nerve-soother that occasionally saves a renegotiation. Tactical tool, not reflex — we deploy accordingly.
