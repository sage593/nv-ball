What to expect from a home inspection in Nevada

A general home inspection is a trained generalist's visual survey of the house's systems — roof, structure, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, drainage — delivered as a report that catalogs everything, from safety issues to loose doorknobs. What it isn't: a pass/fail verdict, an appraisal, or a guarantee. The craft is reading it proportionally — separating the three items that matter from the forty that are Tuesday maintenance — and negotiating the former without torching the deal over the latter. NV Ball Real Estate Group attends inspections and reads reports with exactly that filter.

Walk the inspection with me

What's covered — and not

Covered visually: roof, foundation visible areas, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, drainage, built-in appliances. Not covered: inside walls, sewer laterals (scope separately), septic and wells (specialist inspections we order in rural deals), pools, and anything requiring disassembly. Add-ons like radon testing bolt on cheaply.

Reading proportionally

Every report lists dozens of items — that's the format, not an alarm. The read that matters: safety and systems (negotiate), deferred maintenance (price-informed), cosmetics (yours now). We mark up every report in those three colors.

Negotiating the right things

Repairs, credits, or price — aimed at the items that are genuinely the seller's to own. Nickel-and-diming cosmetics costs goodwill you may need for the real ask; and when competition pressures the contingency, calibrated middles beat waiving.

Straight answers

What does a home inspection cover in Nevada?

Visual survey of roof, structure, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, drainage, built-ins. NOT inside walls, sewer laterals, wells/septic (specialists handle those, standard in rural deals). Radon and other add-ons bolt on cheap.

The report lists 40 problems — should I panic?

No — the catalog-everything format IS the format. Three colors: safety/systems (negotiate), deferred maintenance (informs price), cosmetics (your weekends). Untranslated reports kill deals; translation is the job.

What should I actually ask the seller to fix?

Safety and failing systems — what any owner faces this year. Few asks, documented, priced, as repairs or credits. Skip cosmetic nickel-and-diming; goodwill is a currency.
