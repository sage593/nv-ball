For owners · The fork in the road

Should I rent out or sell my house in Reno?

It depends on four things most owners haven't put side by side: what the home nets as a sale, what it truly cash-flows as a rental (after vacancies, maintenance, and management — not the Zillow-rent daydream), whether you want to be a landlord, and the tax clock — the primary-residence capital-gains exclusion can expire if you convert to a rental and wait too long. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs both versions of the math and tells you which one your situation actually supports.

Run both numbers for me

The rental math, honestly

Gross rent is a fiction until you subtract vacancy, maintenance, management, insurance, and the roof's birthday. We model the real number — some homes cash-flow beautifully; many only pretend to.

The tax clock

Sell a longtime primary residence and the federal exclusion may shelter the gain; convert it to a rental and years later that shelter can lapse. The sequencing is CPA territory — we flag it before you drift past the window.

The landlord question

Tenants call at 2am, Nevada has real landlord-tenant law, and management costs money. Some owners thrive on it. If you'd rather not, we also sell rentals with tenants in place when you're done.

Straight answers

Should I rent out my house or sell it?

Four numbers and one fact: sale net today, TRUE rental cash flow (after vacancy/maintenance/management), what else your equity could do, the tax-exclusion clock — and whether you want 2am tenant calls. The spreadsheet usually answers fast; we run it free.

What do accidental landlords get wrong?

Hope-priced rent, no management until the first crisis, and a blown tax window on the former home. All avoidable — if the math happens before the first lease.

Can you manage the rental if I keep it?

We sell; we don't manage — but we'll refer managers we trust. And when you're done landlording, we're the exit call, tenants in place or not.
