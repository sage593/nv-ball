Relocation · The tax question everyone asks

Capital gains when you sell a California home and move to Nevada

The question arrives with nearly every Bay Area and California mover: what happens to the gain on my house? The short version: the federal primary-residence exclusion ($250,000 single, $500,000 married, owned-and-lived-in two of the last five years) does the heavy lifting; California taxes gain on California real estate regardless of where you live when it closes; and Nevada — with no state income tax — adds nothing on top. The sequencing details belong to your CPA. NV Ball Real Estate Group coordinates the real estate so the timeline they bless is the one that happens.

Coordinate my sale and move

The federal exclusion first

Two-of-five-year rule, $250k/$500k — for most primary-residence sellers this is the whole ballgame. Investment and rental property plays by different rules; a 1031 exchange may apply there.

California's long arm, scoped

California taxes California-source gain and withholds on non-resident sales — but it does not tax your Nevada paycheck or your Nevada home's future appreciation once you're properly domiciled. Residency done right draws that line.

Nevada's zero

No state income tax means your proceeds land whole at the state level and your next decades compound differently — the quiet math behind the whole tax comparison.

Straight answers

Do I pay capital gains tax if I sell my California home and move to Nevada?

Possibly — the levers: the federal primary-residence exclusion ($250k single / $500k married, two-of-five-year rule) does the heavy lifting; California taxes gain on California property no matter where you live at closing; Nevada adds zero. Sequencing is CPA territory — we coordinate with yours.

Should I sell my California house before or after I move?

Moving first doesn't erase California's claim — gain on California property is California-source either way. Real questions: the two-of-five-year exclusion window, non-resident withholding paperwork, and whether you need the equity to buy here. We map it with your CPA.

Does Nevada tax the money I bring from a California home sale?

No. Nevada has no state income tax — your proceeds land untaxed at the state level. Settle the federal bill and California's claim under its rules, and Nevada leaves the rest alone.
