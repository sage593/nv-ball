What is escrow — and how does it work in Nevada?

Escrow is the deal's neutral referee: a company that holds everyone's money and documents until the contract's conditions are met, and releases them exactly as instructed — no sooner, no other way. In Nevada this runs through escrow/title companies rather than attorneys. It opens with your signed contract, holds the earnest money, choreographs lender and payoff logistics, and finishes the sale by recording the deed with the county. That recording — not the signing — is the moment the home changes hands.

Straight answers

What is escrow and how does it work in Nevada?

Escrow is the neutral referee: a company (in Nevada, usually combined with title) that holds funds and documents until the contract's conditions are met, then records the deed and releases money exactly as instructed. Nobody — buyer, seller, or agent — touches the other side's funds directly.

Who chooses the escrow company?

Negotiable, often customary — and worth caring about. A responsive escrow officer is the difference between a smooth closing and a stressful one; we know which offices answer their phones.

What does 'closing' actually mean in Nevada?

Not the signing — the recording. The county recording the deed is the finish line, usually the same day funds land. Then keys.
