Relocation · The SoCal exit

Moving from Southern California to Reno: the honest version

LA, San Diego, and Orange County households keep arriving in Northern Nevada for the same ledger: no state income tax, housing dollars that go visibly further, and mountain seasons in place of coastal sameness. The honest other side — we'd rather you hear it now — is that the ocean doesn't move with you, winter is real, and Reno is a twenty-minute metro, not a scaled-down LA. The movers who thrive here wanted the change. NV Ball Real Estate Group helps you decide if you do.

Talk through the move

The trade, plainly

Ocean for alpine lake, freeway hours for minutes, coastal climate for four sharp seasons, California's tax bill for Nevada's absence of one. Each line cuts both ways; we'll walk your version of the ledger.

Master-planned Damonte Ranch and Somersett for the HOA-polished familiar; Midtown for the walkable; the luxury corridor for view estates at non-coastal numbers.

Straight answers

Why are Southern Californians moving to Reno?

The ledger: no state income tax, housing dollars that visibly stretch, mountains and four seasons, a 20-minute metro. The other side, honestly: no ocean, real winters, and a 7–8-hour drive (or a short flight) back to see everyone.

How does Reno compare to LA or San Diego for daily life?

Smaller and slower on purpose. Commutes in minutes, Tahoe in forty-five, a Midtown scene that surprises people. It is not a mini-LA — the movers who thrive wanted the change, not a replica.

What do SoCal movers underestimate about Northern Nevada?

Winter (snow tires are not optional equipment), high-desert dryness after coastal air, and grieving the ocean — until the first summer afternoon at a Tahoe beach up the hill. We brief movers on the unglamorous specifics.
