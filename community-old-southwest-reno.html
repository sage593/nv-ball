What living here is like

Old Southwest is Reno's architecture museum that people live in: Tudors, Craftsmans, and brick colonials under a century-old canopy, on a walkable grid minutes from the river, downtown, and Midtown's table reservations. Inventory turns over slowly because owners stay — the quarter inspires that — and the homes reward stewards who treat knob-and-tube discoveries as chapters, not crises. For character-first buyers, nothing else in the city argues.

Who Old Southwest Reno fits

Strong fit: character devotees — the buyers who say 'original hardwood' with reverence; walkability-first professionals working downtown or at the hospitals; preservation-minded stewards; anyone for whom Midtown's energy plus a quiet grand block equals the whole brief.

Honest considerations: century homes carry century homework — wiring, plumbing, foundations, and sewer laterals deserve the full inspection suite; renovation budgets and (on some blocks) historic sensibilities apply; garages and closets run period-sized; and scarce inventory demands patience plus decisiveness in the same buyer.

Old Southwest Reno FAQ

Is Old Southwest Reno a good place to live?

For character-first buyers it isn't just good — it's the standard the rest of the city gets compared to. Bring inspection rigor and patience; receive the canopy.

What should buyers inspect in century homes?

Everything with a vintage: wiring, plumbing, foundation, sewer lateral — the full suite, always. Honestly inspected and priced, these homes repay stewards for decades.

How competitive is the market there?

Scarce supply, loyal owners, decisive sales. We prep financing first and tour fast — the only drill that works on these blocks.
