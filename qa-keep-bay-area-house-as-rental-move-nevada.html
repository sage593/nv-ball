Relocation · The keep-or-sell fork

Should you keep your Bay Area house as a rental when moving to Nevada?

The fork every equity-rich mover faces, honestly run: the keep case is real — a Prop-13-protected tax basis you can never get back once sold, Bay Area rents against that frozen basis, long-run appreciation exposure to a supply-starved market — and so is the sell case: the primary-residence capital-gains exclusion has a clock (sell within the window and shelter the gain; convert to a rental and years later that shelter can lapse), landlording from 200 miles away is a real job or a real management fee, and concentrated equity in one California asset is a portfolio decision wearing a house costume. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs the Nevada end either way — here's the fork mapped.

Map my keep-or-sell

The keep case, fairly

Prop 13's frozen tax basis is genuinely irreplaceable — sold once, gone forever; Bay rents against that basis often cash-flow where new landlords couldn't; and the appreciation exposure to a supply-starved market is a legitimate hold thesis. Movers with management tolerance and diversified balance sheets keep defensibly.

The sell case, fairly

The capital-gains exclusion's two-of-five-year clock starts mattering the day you move out — convert to a rental and the shelter can lapse within a few years, which on Bay-sized gains is serious money. Add distance-landlording's reality (management fees or midnight calls), California's continued tax reach on the rental income, and concentration risk. Movers who want clean breaks sell defensibly.

The deciding questions

Three sort most cases: would you buy this house today as a pure investment at its current value (the honest test)? Does your Nevada plan need the equity (the deployment math)? And does the exclusion clock's math — CPA territory, timed precisely — tip your gain? We coordinate both ends with your CPA's timeline.

Straight answers

Should I sell my Bay Area house or rent it out when I move to Nevada?

Both defensible: keep = irreplaceable Prop-13 basis + supply-starved-market exposure; sell = the exclusion clock (lapses years after converting — big money on Bay gains), no distance-landlording, no concentration. Test: would you BUY it today as an investment? CPA times the clock.

How long can I rent out my old house before losing the capital-gains exclusion?

Roughly three years after moving out, under the two-of-five rule — with details (partial exclusions, depreciation recapture) needing CPA precision. Decide before the clock does.

What does managing a Bay Area rental from Reno actually involve?

A management fee or your own tenant-call relationship — plus California taxes on the income, tenant-protective local rules, and known-house maintenance. Done well often; discovered accidentally, never ideal.
