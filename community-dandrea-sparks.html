What living here is like

D'Andrea climbs the hills of east Sparks in tiers of established homes, parks, and valley views that improve with every street you rise. Built as a master plan around a golf course whose later closure became a long-running neighborhood land-use story, the community settled into what it actually is: a well-located hillside suburb where sunsets over the Truckee Meadows are the standing amenity, Sparks' services sit ten minutes down the hill, and the I-80-to-USA-Parkway run makes it one of the easiest addresses in the region for TRIC and Gigafactory commuters.

Who D'Andrea fits

Strong fit: TRIC-corridor commuters who want established-neighborhood life with the easiest eastern run; view buyers on suburban budgets — the upper benches deliver; families using Sparks' parks-and-rec backbone; and buyers who like a settled HOA community with its drama already history.

Honest considerations: the former golf course's open-space status is the neighborhood's known question — we brief every buyer on where it currently stands rather than letting a listing gloss it; hillside streets mean winter ice respect on the upper tiers; east-wind days are real on the exposed benches; and the commute west through the Sparks funnel shares everyone's rush hour.

D'Andrea FAQ

Is D'Andrea a good place to live?

For views-plus-commute buyers — genuinely. Established hillside, parks, sunset views, easiest TRIC run going. Honest items: the former course's land-use story (we brief it straight), upper-tier winter ice, and everyone's shared rush hour west.

What happened to the golf course?

Closed years ago; the land's future became the neighborhood's long-running question. Status moves — so we brief buyers against the live facts, not a page that could go stale.

How is the TRIC commute from D'Andrea?

Among the region's best — I-80 east to USA Parkway, commonly 20–25 minutes, no cross-town slog. For Gigafactory households, that's the quiet superpower.
