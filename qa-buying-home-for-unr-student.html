For parent-buyers · The college play

Buying a home for your college student in Reno: the UNR playbook

The play has a name — the 'kiddie condo' — and real logic: instead of paying rent near the University of Nevada for four-plus years, parents buy a property the student lives in (often with rent-paying roommates covering much of the note), building equity through the degree and exiting — sale or rental conversion — at graduation. The math frequently works near UNR; the execution details decide everything: financing structure (owner-occupant programs with the student on the loan versus straight investment loans), the roommate-landlord reality, century-old-housing diligence in the District, and the exit plan written before the entrance. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs this play most years — here's the honest version.

Run the four-year math

The structures that work

Co-borrowing with your student occupying can open owner-occupied financing (lender rules and honesty both matter — structures get blessed by the lender, not improvised); straight investment purchases keep it simpler at investment rates. Your lender and CPA pick the lane; we find the property that fits it.

The property logic

The University District's cottages and small multifamily are the classic play — walkable, rentable, forever-demanded — with century-home diligence as the price of admission. Newer condos trade character for predictability; duplexes make the roommate math explicit. Each works; budgets and stomachs decide.

The exit, planned at entry

Graduation options: sell into the perpetual student-market demand, convert to a pure rental (the District's depth makes this natural), or keep it as the family's Reno foothold. The tax treatment differs by path — CPA territory — and the best exits were planned four years before they executed.

Straight answers

Should I buy a condo or house for my college student in Reno?

Near UNR, the math frequently works: rent redirected to equity, roommates covering much of the note, perpetual student demand at exit. Execution decides: lender-blessed structure, old-house diligence, roommate reality, exit planned at purchase.

How does financing work when parents buy for a student?

Two lanes: co-borrow with the student occupying (owner-occupied terms, lender rules taken seriously) or straight investment purchase. Lender picks on your facts — improvised occupancy stories unravel badly.

What happens when my student graduates?

Three exits: sell into perpetual demand, convert to rental, or keep the foothold. Tax treatment differs by path — have the CPA conversation at purchase, not graduation.
