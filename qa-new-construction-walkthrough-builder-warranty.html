For buyers · The new-build finish line

Blue tape and the builder warranty: finishing a new build right

New-construction buyers get two quality gates most never use fully: the blue-tape walkthrough before closing — you (and ideally your own inspector) walk the finished home marking every defect with painter's tape, from paint flaws to doors that don't latch, and the builder corrects the punch list, with follow-up walks common and normal — and the builder's first-year workmanship warranty, whose quiet best practice is the 11-month inspection: a professional inspection scheduled about a month before the warranty expires, converting a year of settling cracks, seasonal HVAC performance and small failures into one documented claim while the builder still owes the fixes. Between them: document everything (warranty terms, punch lists with completion dates, reports — one file). Our new-build representation runs both gates; here's the playbook.

Run my new-build gates

The blue-tape method

Walk slowly, mark everything (the tape is the punch list — paint, caulk, scratched glass, sticking doors, grout gaps; petty is the point), test what moves (every window, door, faucet, outlet, appliance cycle), and bring your own inspector — builder quality control is real but works for the builder. Follow-up walks are normal; closing before corrections finishes deserves a written completion commitment.

The first-year rhythm

New homes settle through their first full season cycle: hairline cracks appear, doors adjust, systems reveal themselves in weather they haven't met yet. Keep a running list rather than calling monthly (emergencies excepted — those call now), because the year ends with the move that matters: the 11-month professional inspection, documenting everything warrantied while the warranty still stands. One claim, professionally evidenced, beats twelve phone calls.

The documentation file

Warranty terms (what's covered for one year versus longer structural coverage — read, not assumed), punch lists with dated completions, inspection reports, and every claim in writing. Builders' warranty departments respond to documentation; the incentives guide and new-versus-resale math round out the new-build file.

Straight answers

What is a blue-tape walkthrough on a new construction home?

The pre-closing defect hunt: tape every flaw (petty is the point), builder corrects the punch list, follow-ups are normal. Bring your own inspector; test everything; get unfinished items committed in writing.

What is an 11-month warranty inspection?

A professional inspection ~month eleven — right before the first-year warranty expires — turning the year's settling and system issues into one documented, still-covered claim. Calendar it at closing.

Do I need my own inspector for new construction?

Yes — code inspections verify minimums and builder QC isn't yours. Schedule: pre-drywall where allowed, blue-tape, 11-month. Independent eyes finish the job.
