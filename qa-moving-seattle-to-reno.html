Relocation · The PNW exit

Moving from Seattle to Reno: what to know

Seattle movers keep landing in Reno for sunshine, housing dollars that stretch, and the Sierra out the window — with one honest flag most guides skip: Washington has no state income tax either, so unlike California movers, you're not escaping one. Your case is lifestyle and cost-of-housing math. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs that math against your actual Seattle basis and tells you plainly whether it clears.

Run my Seattle-to-Reno math

What you gain

Roughly 250 sunny days, a metro you cross in twenty minutes, Tahoe in forty-five, and skiing that doesn't require a 5am Snoqualmie gamble. The outdoors here isn't a weekend plan; it's the commute view.

What changes

High desert after maritime green — dry air, sharp seasons, smoke awareness in summer. A smaller scene than Capitol Hill's. The movers who thrive wanted the trade, not a mini-Seattle.

Where Seattle movers land

Walkable Midtown for the neighborhood feel, Somersett and Galena for trail-out-the-door living — the relocation guide maps the rest.

Straight answers

Why are people moving from Seattle to Reno?

The pattern: no state income tax, housing dollars that stretch, ~250 sunny days against the gray, and the Sierra out your window. Honest trade-offs: a much smaller metro, high desert after all that green, and smoke-season awareness in summer.

How does Reno compare to Seattle for daily life?

Smaller, sunnier, drier, and crossable in twenty minutes — no Mercer mess. Thinner tech-and-coffee scene; absurdly better ski-hike-climb access. The happy movers wanted mountains and margin, not Seattle at 80% scale.

Does the no-income-tax math work for Washington movers?

Honest flag: Washington has no state income tax either — you're not escaping one. Your Reno case is housing cost, sun, and Sierra access. Run the full picture; don't assume a tax windfall that isn't there.
