Buying a short sale in Nevada: the patience trade, explained

A short sale is a purchase with a silent third party: the seller owes more than the home's worth, so their lender must approve taking less — which means your accepted offer starts a waiting game (commonly months, not weeks) with a counterparty who answers on bank time, may counter late, and can say no after the wait. The payoff for the right buyer: thinner competition and pricing that compensates patience. The wrong buyer — anyone on a deadline, in a rate lock, or emotionally spent by week six — should be steered to bank-owned listings instead, where distressed pricing comes with normal timelines. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs both lanes; here's the honest sort.

Sort my distressed lane

How the wait actually works

Your offer goes to the seller, then to their lender's loss-mitigation machinery: valuation, negotiator assignment, investor sign-offs — months of process you can't accelerate, punctuated by document requests and occasional late counters. Approval is genuinely uncertain until issued; plans built on it are built on sand.

Who the trade fits

Patient buyers with flexible timelines (renters month-to-month, investors, second-home shoppers), rate plans that tolerate the wait, and the temperament to treat silence as normal. The compensation: thinner competition and pricing that generally respects the hassle. Deadline buyers belong in REO or standard listings — no shame, just sorting.

Protecting yourself in the wait

Offer terms that keep your options alive (approval deadlines with exit rights, deposits that stay safe pre-approval), continued shopping where the contract allows, and diligence timed to post-approval so you're not buying inspections for a maybe. We write short-sale offers for exactly this asymmetry.

Straight answers

How long does a short sale take to buy in Nevada?

Months, commonly: lender loss-mitigation runs on bank time, counters late, and can decline after the wait. Patience gets paid in thinner competition and pricing; deadline buyers belong in REO instead.

Can the bank reject my short-sale offer after the seller accepted?

Yes — the seller's yes starts the process; the lender's is the real gate, months away, and sometimes a counter or a no. Write terms that price that: exit rights, protected deposits, freedom to shop.

Are short sales good deals?

Sometimes — the discount pays for patience and uncertainty, not magic. Compare against REO (distressed price, normal timeline) and tired-listing negotiation. Temperament sorts the lane.
