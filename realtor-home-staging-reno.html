Is home staging worth it in Reno, NV?

Often — but not always, and anyone quoting a universal return-on-staging statistic is selling you something. Staging earns its cost when an empty home photographs poorly, a floor plan confuses, or the segment's buyers shop on emotion; it matters least on clean, in-demand homes that already show well. NV Ball Real Estate Group walks every listing room by room and gives a plain verdict: stage this, skip that, and often — just clean, declutter, and repaint.

Get my room-by-room verdict

When staging pays

Vacant homes that echo in photos, unusual floor plans that need explaining, and segments — move-up, luxury — where buyers purchase a feeling. There, good staging is cheap against the price it protects.

When to skip it

Clean, well-lit, sensibly-arranged homes in hungry segments don't need rented furniture. We'd rather you spend nothing than spend on theater — the photos will tell us which home you have.

The prep that always pays

Deep cleaning, decluttering, targeted paint, and photography timed to your home's best light. It's most of what great listings share — timing helps too.

What does staging cost, and who pays?

Scope decides — a consult plus key rooms is one budget; furnishing a vacant house for months is another. Sellers typically pay, which is why we only point you there when the math favors you. Quotes come from stagers we trust, not from us.

What's the alternative if I don't stage?

The unglamorous work that often beats furniture: deep clean, declutter, paint where it counts, matching light bulbs, photos timed to your best light. Most listing prep is exactly this — cheap, boring, effective.
