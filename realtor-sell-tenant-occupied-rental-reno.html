Selling a rental property with tenants in Reno, NV

You can sell a tenant-occupied rental in Nevada — the lease transfers to the buyer, it doesn't end at closing. The real questions are tactical: lawful notice before showings, whether occupied or vacant nets you more, and keeping the tenant cooperative through escrow. NV Ball Real Estate Group handles occupied sales regularly, with 93 homes sold in the last twelve months across every situation a landlord can hand us.

The occupied-vs-vacant math

A reliable tenant on a solid lease draws investors; a vacant, refreshed home draws owner-occupants and often a higher net. We run both scenarios — with real numbers from your property, not theory.

Tenant diplomacy

Showings need lawful advance notice and a tenant who doesn't sabotage them. Clear written communication, respectful scheduling, and sometimes an incentive keep the home showable and the sale smooth.

Investor-ready packaging

Lease, payment history, and deposit paperwork assembled the way investor buyers expect — and if the better buyer is an owner-occupant, we'll tell you what that path requires instead. Thinking of trading up? A 1031 exchange may fit.

Straight answers

Can I sell my Reno rental with tenants still living in it?

Yes — the lease transfers to the buyer; it doesn't die at closing. We sell occupied rentals regularly. The craft is lawful showing notice, a tenant plan that keeps cooperation, and the honest occupied-vs-vacant math.

Should I sell occupied or wait until the tenant leaves?

Honest answer: it depends. Solid tenant + solid lease = investor appeal. Vacant + refreshed = owner-occupant appeal, often a higher net. We run both numbers before you pick.

What happens to the security deposit and the lease at closing?

Deposit transfers with the sale (or returns to the tenant); the lease rides along on its terms. Escrow papers it; we keep the tenant informed in writing so the last week stays calm.
