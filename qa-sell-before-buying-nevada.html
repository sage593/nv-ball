Buying & selling · Strategy

Should I sell my house before buying another?

It depends on your equity, your loan options, and this month's market — and most Northern Nevada move-up buyers land on one of three strategies: buying contingent on their sale, negotiating a rent-back that bridges the gap after closing, or qualifying to carry both homes briefly. Each has a price and a comfort level. The mistake is choosing by default instead of running your actual numbers.

Talk to an agent

Contingent offers

Cleanest risk profile, weakest negotiating posture — how weak depends on the week. We'll tell you what the market currently tolerates.

The rent-back bridge

Sell, close, stay put for a negotiated period, then move once into the new home. Often the sanest path, and very negotiable here.

Carrying both

Strongest offers, biggest bank requirements. Worth pricing with a lender even if just to rule it out — sometimes it's closer than you think.

Straight answers

Should I sell my house before buying another in Nevada?

Most of our move-up clients coordinate both as one plan — contingent offers, rent-backs to bridge the gap, or briefly carrying both when the numbers allow. The wrong answer is deciding by default. Fifteen minutes with your actual numbers settles it.

What is a rent-back and does it work here?

You sell, close, and stay put for a negotiated period while you land the next home. Common here, and often the cleanest bridge — we negotiate them regularly.

Will sellers accept an offer contingent on my sale?

Depends on the week and the house — sometimes easily, sometimes with sweeteners. That's a live-market read, and we'll give it to you straight before you write.
