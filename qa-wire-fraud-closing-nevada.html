Wire fraud at closing: how to protect your money in Nevada

The scam is brutally simple: criminals compromise email threads around a closing, then send wire instructions that look exactly right — title company's name, your file number, perfect timing — routing your down payment or sale proceeds to their account. It's rising industry-wide, increasingly voice-cloned and AI-polished, and the defense is a habit, not a product: verify wire instructions by phone using a number you already had, never one from the email, every single time. NV Ball Real Estate Group builds that verification step into every closing we touch — here's the whole protocol.

Lock down my closing

The protocol

Get wire instructions once, early, through the title company's secure channel; verify by phone on a number from their website or your first in-person meeting — never from the email; treat ANY change to instructions as fraud until a human you know confirms otherwise. Changes are the tell: legitimate instructions almost never change.

The red flags

Last-minute 'updated' instructions, urgency language, slightly-off email addresses, and calls that sound right (voice cloning is real now — verify numbers, not voices). When closing week arrives, paranoia is professionalism.

If money moved

Minutes matter: call your bank's fraud line immediately and request a recall, call the title company, and file with the FBI's IC3 the same hour. Fast action recovers funds sometimes; slow action almost never. We keep this escalation list in every closing packet.

Straight answers

How does wire fraud happen at real estate closings?

Compromised email + perfect-looking instructions at exactly the right moment. Defense is a habit: verify by phone on a number you ALREADY had, never from the email — and treat any changed instructions as fraud until a known human confirms.

What should I do if I wired money to a scammer?

Minutes matter: bank fraud line + recall request, title company alerted, FBI IC3 complaint — all in the first hour. Fast sometimes recovers; slow almost never does.

How does your team protect clients from this?

Built-in: the protocol briefed at contract, instructions through secure channels once, and the warning repeated in closing week — when the fake email actually arrives. Nobody legitimate ever rushes your verification call.
