Moving your aging parents near you: the Reno playbook

The call that starts it comes eventually — and the housing spectrum here serves the whole arc: independent-but-near (the townhome or single-level purchase minutes from you — the retirement map's lock-and-leave tier), the shared-property versions (ADUs and next-gen suites where lots and codes allow — the fastest-growing answer), the 55+ communities' supported independence (calendars built in), and the honest conversation about assisted tiers when the arc reaches them (we're realtors, not care planners — but the housing timeline and the care timeline deserve joint planning). Purchase structures (who buys, whose name, co-ownership paperwork) round out the file. NV Ball Real Estate Group runs these family projects with the patience they deserve.

Plan the family move

The spectrum, honestly

Independent-near: single-level, low-maintenance, minutes-from-you — the move that extends independence by years. Shared-property: ADUs and suites (lot and code homework ours) — proximity with doors. Community-supported: 55+ calendars and single-level design. Each tier buys time; the family conversation picks the entry point.

The structures that work

Who buys matters: parents purchasing (their estate, their basis — vesting counsel applies), adult children purchasing (occupancy honesty with lenders, co-ownership agreements in writing), or blends the attorneys structure. The paperwork conversation before the house hunt saves the awkward version during escrow.

The conversations first

The timeline talk (what the move serves now versus the arc's later stages), the finance talk (whose money, whose name, whose estate), and the dignity talk (parents choosing versus being arranged — the moves that work are theirs). We've learned to ask; the families who talk first shop better.

Straight answers

How do I move my aging parents closer to me in Reno?

Pick the spectrum entry: independent-near (single-level minutes away), shared-property (ADUs/suites), or 55+ supported independence — structures (who buys, whose name) settled first. Talk timeline, finances, dignity before shopping.

Should my parents buy the house or should I?

Facts decide: parents buying = clean estate/basis; children buying = lender honesty + written co-ownership; blends = attorney-structured. Before escrow, always.

What homes work best for aging parents?

The durable list: single-level, low-maintenance, near you and care, aging-in-place bones (wide doors, walk-in showers, stepless entry). Standard filters here.
